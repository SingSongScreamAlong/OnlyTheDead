# VERDUN — FEBRUARY 1916
## Detailed Timeline: The Opening of Hell

**Phase**: I — The Opening Bombardment & Initial German Offensive
**Duration**: February 1-29, 1916 (29 days)
**Key Battles**: Bois des Caures, Fort Douaumont, Bois d'Haumont, Herbebois, Wavrille
**Strategic Objective**: German Operation "Gericht" (Judgment) — Break French Army at Verdun
**Weather**: Winter conditions, snow early month, thaw mid-month, mud late month
**Result**: German territorial gains; Fort Douaumont captured; French line holds at critical moment; Pétain assumes command

---

## FEBRUARY 1, 1916 (Pre-Battle: Day -20)

### German Preparations (Hidden)
- Crown Prince Wilhelm's 5th Army continues covert buildup
- Railway construction accelerating behind German lines
- 1,220 artillery pieces now in position (target: 1,400 by Feb 12)
- Ammunition stockpiles: 2,500,000 shells accumulated

### French Sector (Verdun)
- RFV (Région Fortifiée de Verdun) under General Herr
- Garrison strength: ~270,000 men (across entire sector)
- Front-line troops (right bank): ~34,000 men in three divisions
  - 51st Division (Gen. Boulangé) — Bois des Caures sector
  - 72nd Division (Gen. Bapst) — Herbebois-Wavrille sector
  - 14th Division (Gen. Thévenet) — Fort Douaumont-Vaux sector

### Fort Douaumont Status
- Garrison: 57 elderly Territorial soldiers (maintenance crew)
- Artillery: 1x 155mm turret (operational), 1x 75mm turret (jammed)
- Strategic value: Dominates terrain (elev. 388m), concrete fortress
- **Critical vulnerability**: Skeleton crew since artillery withdrawn Nov 1915

### Weather
- Cold, clear
- Temperature: -4°C to 2°C
- Snow cover: 15-20cm
- Ground: Frozen hard

### Intelligence
- French intelligence reports German activity but underestimates scale
- Aerial reconnaissance hampered by winter weather
- GQG (French High Command) focused on Somme offensive preparations
- Joffre dismisses Verdun threat: "Nothing to fear"

### 07:15 — **THE BOMBARDMENT BEGINS**

#### 07:15:00 — First Salvo
- **EXACT TIME RECORDED**: 07:15 and 30 seconds

- Target: French command posts, communication centers
- Sound heard 30km away in Bar-le-Duc

- Emerges from rubble, uninjured

- Sends runner to battalion HQ (runner killed 50m away)

---

### 07:30-12:00 — THE INFERNO (PHASE 1: DESTRUCTION)

#### Artillery Characteristics
- Rate of fire: ~100,000 shells per hour (average)
- Concentration: Heaviest bombardment in human history (to date)
- Sound: Continuous roar, individual explosions indistinguishable
- Effect: Earth trembles; felt in dugouts 10km away

#### 08:00 — Pattern Bombardment Begins
- German artillery shifts to systematic area coverage
- Fire walks back and forth across French trenches
- Each trench line receives 30-minute bombardment, then repeats
- Dugouts collapsing from direct hits and cumulative shock

#### Bois des Caures (08:00-12:00)
- Driant's men in deep shelters (dugouts 3-4m underground)
- Direct hits collapsing some dugouts (men buried alive)
- No communication with outside world (all runners killed)
- Men can only wait in darkness, praying

#### French Casualties (Morning)
- Killed by shell fire: ~2,000 (estimated, morning only)
- Buried alive: ~500 (many will suffocate or die of wounds)
- Wounded in dugouts: ~1,500 (many untreatable - cannot evacuate)
- Shell shock casualties: ~3,000 (incapacitated, unable to fight)

#### French Artillery Response (Ineffective)
- French 75mm batteries attempt counter-battery fire
- German artillery positions too far back (out of 75mm range)
- French heavy artillery (155mm) limited in number
- By 10:00: Most French batteries silenced (destroyed or crews dead)

---

### 12:00-16:00 — THE INFERNO (PHASE 2: FRONT LINES)

#### 12:00 — Focus Shifts to Trenches
- German fire concentrates on front-line trenches

- Shrapnel shells: anti-personnel effect

#### Bois des Caures (12:00-14:00)

- Driant's shelters holding (built well)
- Some men going mad from noise and concussion
- Driant moves through shelters, encouraging men: "Hold fast, the infantry will come, and we will stop them"

#### 14:00 — German Probing Attacks (Premature)

- Appear through smoke/dust (visibility <10 meters)
- Some French positions silent (all occupants dead)
- Others open fire (survivors still fighting)

#### 14:30 — Bombardment Intensifies Again
- Focus on identified resistance points
- Driant's sector receives special attention (known strongpoint)
- 420mm "Big Bertha" shells hit Bois des Caures
- Craters 10 meters wide, 4 meters deep

### 16:00-17:00 — FINAL HOUR

#### 16:00 — Crescendo

## FEBRUARY 27, 1916 — DAY 7: LA VOIE SACRÉE

### **KEY EVENT: THE SACRED WAY ESTABLISHED**

#### The Supply Problem
- Verdun sector: 200,000+ troops (and growing)
- Supply needs: 2,000 tons per day (food, ammunition, equipment)
- Railway: Cut by German artillery (unusable)
- Only road: Bar-le-Duc to Verdun (75km, narrow country road)

#### Pétain's Solution
- Transform Bar-le-Duc road into major supply artery
- Assign: 8,500 Territorial soldiers to road maintenance
- System: Continuous truck convoy (24 hours/day)
- Speed limit: 15 km/h (to reduce accidents)
- Spacing: 14 meters between trucks

#### Feb 27 — Voie Sacrée Begins Operations
- First organized convoy departs Bar-le-Duc (06:00)
- 300 trucks, carrying ammunition
- Arrives Verdun outskirts (18:00)
- Return trip overnight (empty trucks)
- System established

#### The Name: "La Voie Sacrée" (The Sacred Way)
- Name given by Maurice Barrès (writer, journalist)
- Sacred because: Thousands of trucks carrying supplies, hundreds of thousands of men march upon it to battle
- Becomes symbol of French determination

#### Statistics (Eventually)
- Trucks: 3,900 in rotation
