# THE VERDUN TIMELINE
## Complete Chronicle of the Battle of Verdun
### February 21 – December 18, 1916

---

## OVERVIEW

**Duration**: 303 days (10 months)
**Combatants**: French Army vs. German Army (Imperial German Army)
**Location**: Verdun-sur-Meuse, northeastern France
**Result**: French strategic victory at devastating cost
**Casualties**:
- French: ~377,231 (163,000 dead or missing)
- German: ~337,000 (143,000 dead or missing)
- Total: ~714,000+ casualties

---

## PHASE I: THE STORM BREAKS
### February 21 – March 6, 1916

#### February 21, 1916 — Day 1: "Operation Gericht"
**Weather**: Cold, snow flurries, visibility poor
**Time**: 07:15 - Artillery barrage begins
**Duration of bombardment**: 9 hours
**Artillery**: 1,220 German guns across 20km front
**Shells fired**: ~1,000,000 in first 12 hours
**Primary targets**:
- Bois des Caures
- Herbebois
- Haumont Wood
- Brabant

**Key Events**:
- 07:15: German artillery opens "Trommelfeuer" (drumfire)
- 16:00: Infantry assault begins (delayed by snow)
- Col. Émile Driant commands 56th and 59th Chasseurs Battalions in Bois des Caures
- French observation posts destroyed within first hour
- Communication lines severed
- German assault troops advance with flamethrowers

**Units Engaged**:
- **German**: XVIII Corps (von Zwehl), III Corps (von Lochow), V Reserve Corps
- **French**: XXX Corps (Chrétien), 51st and 72nd Divisions

**Terrain**: Forested ridges, frozen mud, pre-existing French trench systems

**Coordinates**: 49.1593°N, 5.3845°E (Bois des Caures)

---

#### February 22, 1916 — Day 2: Fall of Bois des Caures
**Weather**: Continued cold, intermittent snow
**Casualties**: ~6,000 French on Day 1-2

**Key Events**:
- Morning: Col. Driant killed during German assault on Bois des Caures
- Afternoon: Bois des Caures falls to German 6th Division
- German advance reaches Beaumont village
- French 51st Division suffers 60% casualties
- Evacuation of Samogneux begins

**Command Response**:
- Gen. Chrétien requests reinforcements
- Gen. de Castelnau (Chief of Staff) orders XX Corps to Verdun

---

#### February 23-24, 1916 — Days 3-4: German Momentum
**Key Events**:
- Feb 23: Samogneux falls
- Feb 24: German forces reach Louvemont
- Fort Douaumont visible from German lines
- French lines contracting toward right bank of Meuse

---

#### February 25, 1916 — Day 5: The Fall of Douaumont
**Weather**: Clear, cold
**Time**: 16:00

**Critical Event**: Fort Douaumont captured by small detachment of German 24th Brandenburg Regiment without major resistance.

**Context**:
- Fort was inadequately garrisoned (56 territorial artillerists, no combat infantry)
- Heavy guns removed in 1915 for use elsewhere
- Symbolic and strategic disaster for France
- German propaganda triumph

**French Response**:
- National shock
- Gen. Pétain appointed commander of Verdun sector (Feb 26)
- Immediate reorganization of defense

**Coordinates**: 49.1969°N, 5.4361°E (Fort Douaumont)

---

#### February 26, 1916 — Day 6: Pétain Arrives
**Command Change**: General Philippe Pétain assumes command of Second Army at Verdun

**Pétain's Immediate Actions**:
- Reorganizes artillery into centralized command
- Establishes "Noria" (rotation system) for divisions
- Demands restoration of supply line (Bar-le-Duc road → "La Voie Sacrée")
- Orders: "Ils ne passeront pas" (They shall not pass)

**Logistical Feat**:
- La Voie Sacrée: 75km road from Bar-le-Duc to Verdun
- 3,900 trucks
- One vehicle every 14 seconds
- 190,000 tons of supplies per week
- Road maintained by 8,500 territorial soldiers

---

#### February 27 – March 6, 1916: Stabilization
**French Reinforcements Arrive**:
- XX Corps (Balfourier)
- I Colonial Corps
- Additional artillery batteries

**German Attacks Continue**:
- Feb 29: Attack on Fort Vaux (repelled)
- Mar 2: Assault on Mort-Homme begins (west bank)
- Mar 4: German attacks stall along Meuse ridges
- Mar 6: Crown Prince Wilhelm orders pause to consolidate

**Phase I Summary**:
- German territorial gains: ~10km depth, 25km² area
- Fort Douaumont lost
- French lines stabilized under Pétain
- Attrition warfare pattern established

---

## PHASE II: EXPANSION TO LEFT BANK
### March 6 – April 9, 1916

#### March 6, 1916: The Battle for Mort-Homme Begins
**Target**: Hill 295 ("Le Mort-Homme" / Dead Man's Hill)
**Strategic Importance**: Dominates left (west) bank of Meuse, overlooks German supply routes

**Weather**: Rain begins, ground turns to mud
**German Forces**: V Reserve Corps, VI Reserve Corps

**Key Terrain Features**:
- Mort-Homme (Hill 295)
- Hill 304 (Côte 304)
- Cumières
- Chattancourt

---

#### March 14, 1916: Assault on Hill 304
**Objective**: Eliminate French artillery observation on left bank
**German Artillery**: Intensified bombardment
**Result**: Grinding attrition, no breakthrough

---

#### March 20 – April 9, 1916: The Mort-Homme Meat Grinder
**Conditions**:
- Continuous rain
- Trenches collapse into liquid mud
- Wounded drown in shell craters
- Gas attacks (phosgene, chlorine)
- No man's land becomes impassable

**Tactical Pattern**:
- German attacks by day
- French counterattacks by night
- Artillery dominates both sides
- Casualties mount with minimal territory change

**Notable Units**:
- French: 29th Division, 67th Division
- German: 11th Bavarian Division, 50th Division

**Casualty Rate**: ~25,000 per week (both sides combined)

---

## PHASE III: THE MAY INFERNO
### April 9 – June 7, 1916

#### April 9, 1916: Renewed German Offensive on Right Bank
**Target**: Fort Vaux and Froideterre ridge
**German Command**: Crown Prince Wilhelm under pressure from Falkenhayn
**Strategy**: "Bleed France white" (Ausblutung)

---

#### May 3, 1916: Hill 304 Falls
**After 7 weeks of fighting**, German forces capture Hill 304
**Cost**: ~20,000 casualties (German), ~30,000 (French)
**Significance**: Artillery observation advantage shifts

---

#### May 8-20, 1916: Battle for Mort-Homme Intensifies
**Phosgene Gas Attack** (May 8): German gas barrage on Mort-Homme
**Hand-to-hand combat** in collapsed trenches
**May 20**: Germans capture peak of Mort-Homme
**French retain southern slopes**

**Command**:
- Gen. Robert Nivelle commands Verdun sector artillery (replaces Pétain, who moves to higher command)
- Nivelle's doctrine: "Counterattack always"

---

#### May 22 – June 7, 1916: The Assault on Fort Vaux

##### May 22: German Bombardment of Fort Vaux
**Artillery concentration**: 500 guns target fort and surrounding area
**Duration**: 5 days continuous
**Shells fired**: ~8,000 per day on fort alone

##### June 1, 1916: Infantry Assault Begins
**German Forces**: 50th Division
**Fort Commander**: Major Sylvain-Eugène Raynal
**Garrison**: ~600 men (incl. wounded from nearby units)

##### June 2-6: Siege of Fort Vaux
**Conditions inside fort**:
- No water (cistern destroyed)
- Wounded dying of thirst
- German flammenwerfer attacks through galleries
- Hand-to-hand combat in tunnels
- Carrier pigeon "Vaillant" delivers final message (June 4)

**June 7, 1916**: Fort Vaux surrenders
**Major Raynal** taken prisoner with honors

**German Victory**: Symbolic, but tactically exhausted

---

## PHASE IV: THE HIGH WATER MARK
### June 8 – June 23, 1916

#### June 21-23, 1916: The Farthest German Advance
**Operation**: "Green Cross" gas attack (diphosgene)
**Target**: Souville and Thiaumont sectors

**June 23, 1916**: German forces reach **4km from Verdun**
**Closest point**: Fleury-devant-Douaumont
**Situation**: French line near collapse

**Critical Moment**:
- German Crown Prince requests final assault on June 24
- Falkenhayn refuses: troops exhausted
- **July 1**: Battle of the Somme begins (British-French offensive)
- German attention and reserves diverted

**High-Water Mark**:
- Coordinates: 49.1914°N, 5.4197°E (near Thiaumont)
- German offensive **halted**
- Stalemate begins

---

## PHASE V: THE GREAT ATTRITION
### June 23 – October 24, 1916

#### July – August 1916: Stalemate and Decay
**Conditions**:
- Summer heat, decomposing bodies
- Flies, disease, dysentery epidemic
- Artillery duels continue daily
- Localized attacks and counterattacks
- Somme offensive drains German reserves

**Notable**:
- Thiaumont Ouvrage changes hands **16 times** (June-August)
- Fleury village reduced to dust, changes hands repeatedly
- No significant territorial changes

**Weather**: July heat wave, August rain returns

---

#### September – October 1916: French Build Strength
**Command**:
- Gen. Nivelle plans counteroffensive
- French artillery reorganized and reinforced
- New tactics: rolling barrage coordinated with infantry

**German Weakness**:
- Somme casualties mounting
- Verdun now secondary front
- Defensive posture

---

## PHASE VI: THE FRENCH RECLAMATION
### October 24 – December 18, 1916

#### October 24, 1916: The Counteroffensive Begins
**Commander**: Gen. Charles Mangin (under Nivelle)
**Objective**: Recapture Fort Douaumont and Fort Vaux

**Preparation**:
- 740 French guns (incl. heavy naval artillery)
- 4-day bombardment
- Use of creeping barrage tactics
- Colonial troops (Moroccan Division, RICM)

**Weather**: Cold, rain turning to sleet

---

#### October 24, 1916 — Day 1 of Counteroffensive
**Time**: 11:40 - Infantry assault
**Units**:
- Régiment d'Infanterie Coloniale du Maroc (RICM)
- 38th Division
- Moroccan Division

**Results**:
- Rapid advance through devastated terrain
- German resistance disorganized
- **Fort Douaumont liberated by evening**
- 6,000 German prisoners

**Key Figure**: Lt. Col. Régis de Vibraye (RICM)

**Coordinates Recaptured**: 49.1969°N, 5.4361°E (Douaumont)

---

#### November 2, 1916: Fort Vaux Liberated
**Method**: Germans evacuate during night (Nov 1-2)
**Reason**: Position untenable, supply lines cut
**French reoccupy** Nov 2 without resistance

**Symbolic Victory**: Forts returned after 8 months

---

#### November 3 – December 15, 1916: Continued Pressure
**French Advances**:
- Nov 15: Recapture of Bezonvaux
- Dec 2: Louvemont retaken
- Dec 15: Final French offensive pushes Germans back to Feb 21 starting lines

**Weather**: Winter cold, snow, frozen ground

---

#### December 18, 1916: Official End of Battle
**Result**:
- Front line restored approximately to Feb 21, 1916 positions
- French strategic and symbolic victory
- German offensive failed

**Cost**:
- 303 days of combat
- ~714,000 total casualties
- Verdun city damaged but never taken
- Psychological impact: both nations traumatized

---

## AFTERMATH & LEGACY

**French Victory Defined By**:
- "Ils ne passeront pas" fulfilled
- Douaumont and Vaux recaptured
- Verdun city held
- German Crown Prince's offensive defeated

**German Failure Defined By**:
- Strategic objectives unmet
- Attrition goal backfired (German casualties nearly equal French)
- Somme offensive forced resource diversion
- Morale impact on German public

**Long-Term Impact**:
- French Army mutinies (1917) partially rooted in Verdun trauma
- Pétain becomes national hero (later controversial WWII figure)
- "Verdun" becomes synonym for futility of WWI
- Ossuary of Douaumont: 130,000 unidentified remains

---

## TERRAIN TRANSFORMATION

**Before**:
- Forested hills, villages, farmland
- Network of 19th-century forts

**After**:
- Lunar landscape, craters 10m deep
- Forests obliterated (Zone Rouge established)
- Villages erased (9 never rebuilt)
- Soil poisoned by chemicals and explosives
- Human remains still emerging today

**Zone Rouge**: Area remains restricted due to unexploded ordnance and toxic contamination

---

## CULTURAL MEMORY

**French National Identity**:
- "La Voie Sacrée" enshrined
- Douaumont Ossuary and Memorial (1932)
- Annual remembrance ceremonies
- Verdun = symbol of French resilience

**German Perspective**:
- Remembered as costly failure
- Crown Prince Wilhelm's reputation damaged
- Example of Falkenhayn's flawed strategy

**Universal Symbol**:
- Futility of industrial warfare
- Human cost of nationalism
- Endurance and trauma of soldiers
