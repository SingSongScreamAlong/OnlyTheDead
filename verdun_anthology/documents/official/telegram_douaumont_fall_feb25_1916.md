# TELEGRAM: Douaumont Fall Report
**From**: Gen. Frédéric-Georges Herr, Commander, Fortified Region of Verdun
**To**: Gen. Édouard de Castelnau, Chief of General Staff, GQG Chantilly
**Date**: February 25, 1916
**Time Sent**: 18:45 hours
**Classification**: URGENT — SECRET
**Status**: Original archived at Service Historique de la Défense, Vincennes

---

```
URGENT SECRET

FM: GEN HERR / RFV VERDUN
TO: GEN DE CASTELNAU / GQG CHANTILLY
DTG: 251845FEB16

FORT DOUAUMONT LOST STOP GERMAN FORCES CAPTURED FORT APPROX 1600 HRS TODAY STOP

MINIMAL RESISTANCE STOP GARRISON INSUFFICIENT STOP ESTIMATED 50-60 TERRITORIALS ONLY STOP NO COMBAT TROOPS STOP HEAVY GUNS REMOVED 1915 PER YOUR DIRECTIVE STOP

GERMANS EXPLOITING SUCCESS STOP PUSHING TOWARD VAUX AND SOUVILLE STOP FRENCH COUNTERATTACKS REPULSED STOP HEAVY CASUALTIES STOP

REQUEST IMMEDIATE REINFORCEMENTS STOP REQUEST ARTILLERY SUPPORT STOP SITUATION CRITICAL STOP

RECOMMEND COURT OF INQUIRY INTO FORT GARRISON STRENGTH DECISIONS STOP

HERR / COMMANDING / RFV VERDUN

END MSG
```

## TELEGRAM: Civilian Evacuation Order
**From**: Préfet (Prefect) of Meuse Department
**To**: All Mayors, Verdun District
**Date**: February 23, 1916
**Time**: 14:00 hours
**Classification**: OFFICIAL — PUBLIC

---

```
OFFICIAL URGENT

FROM: PREFECTURE MEUSE
TO: ALL MAYORS VERDUN DISTRICT
DATE: 231400FEB16

MANDATORY EVACUATION ORDER STOP

ALL CIVILIANS WITHIN 10 KM OF VERDUN CITADEL MUST EVACUATE IMMEDIATELY STOP

EVACUATION ROUTES: WEST VIA BAR-LE-DUC STOP SOUTH VIA SAINT-MIHIEL STOP

MILITARY TRANSPORT AVAILABLE FOR ELDERLY AND INFIRM STOP

CIVILIANS MAY TAKE ONE SUITCASE PER PERSON STOP LIVESTOCK AND HEAVY GOODS MUST BE ABANDONED STOP

FAILURE TO EVACUATE: CIVILIANS REMAINING AFTER 24 FEB ARE SUBJECT TO MILITARY AUTHORITY STOP NO PROTECTION GUARANTEED STOP

EFFECTIVE: 0600 HRS 24 FEB 1916 STOP

BY ORDER: PREFECTURE MEUSE

END NOTICE
```

**Historical Notes**:

1. **Fort Douaumont Telegram**: Gen. Herr's actual report to GQG (Grand Quartier Général) on Feb 25, 1916 reported Douaumont's fall. He was effectively relieved of command shortly after (Pétain replaced him). The "court of inquiry" reference reflects real tensions—Joffre's decision to disarm forts in 1915 was controversial and contributed to Douaumont's vulnerability.

2. **Pétain Appointment**: Pétain was appointed Feb 26, 1916. His famous "Ils ne passeront pas" was likely not sent via telegram (it was an oral order), but the phrase summarizes his strategic intent.

3. **German Intercept**: French signals intelligence did intercept German communications at Verdun. By 1916, both sides had rudimentary cryptanalysis. German overconfidence (expecting French collapse "within days") was real.

4. **Civilian Evacuation**: Verdun city and surrounding villages were evacuated Feb 23-24. Many civilians fled with only one suitcase. Approximately 3,000 civilians remained in Verdun city throughout the battle (mostly those who refused to leave or were unable to travel).

**Game Integration**:
- **Tutorial**: Telegrams introduce player to communication delays and fog of war
- **Story beats**: Telegrams announce major events (Douaumont fall, Pétain appointment)
- **Found documents**: Telegrams discovered in abandoned HQs, telegraph stations
- **Cutscene**: Visual of telegram being typed, delivered by runner
- **Gameplay mechanic**: Player must send/receive telegrams under time pressure
- **Dramatic irony**: German telegram "expect collapse within days" contrasts with French resilience
- **Historical authenticity**: Short, clipped telegram style (expensive, word limits)
- **Civilian perspective**: Evacuation order shows non-military impact of battle
- **Command perspective**: Shows decision-making at highest levels (Joffre, Pétain)
