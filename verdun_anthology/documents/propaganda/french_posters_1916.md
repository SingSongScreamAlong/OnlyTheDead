# FRENCH PROPAGANDA POSTERS — 1916
## Verdun Campaign

**Source**: Collection of French WWI propaganda (Bibliothèque nationale de France)
**Purpose**: Maintain civilian morale, encourage war bonds, recruitment, industrial production
**Distribution**: Posted in cities, towns, factories, schools, train stations
**Artists**: Various (government-commissioned illustrators)

---

## POSTER #1: "ON LES AURA!"
### ("WE'LL GET THEM!")

**Date**: March 1916
**Size**: 120cm x 80cm
**Artist**: Jules Abel Faivre (attributed)
**Print Run**: 500,000 copies

### VISUAL DESCRIPTION:
- **Central Figure**: French *poilu* (soldier) in horizon-blue uniform
  - Muscular, determined expression
  - Steel helmet (Adrian helmet)
  - Rifle with fixed bayonet raised high
  - Mud-splattered but unbowed
  - Eyes looking upward and forward (toward victory)
- **Background**:
  - Smoke and fire (battlefield)
  - Silhouettes of advancing soldiers
  - French tricolor (red, white, blue) behind soldier
- **Typography**:
  - **Top (large, bold red letters)**: "ON LES AURA!"
  - **Bottom (smaller text)**: "Souscrivez à l'Emprunt National" (Subscribe to the National Loan)
  - **Very bottom**: "Pour assurer la Victoire et une Paix durable" (To ensure Victory and a lasting Peace)

### HISTORICAL CONTEXT:
- Phrase "On les aura!" popularized by Gen. Pétain and Gen. Nivelle
- Became rallying cry for Verdun defense
- Used to sell war bonds (emprunts nationaux)
- Posted throughout France, March-June 1916

### EMOTIONAL TONE:
- Defiant optimism
- Masculine strength
- Inevitability of victory
- Patriotic duty (buying bonds = supporting soldiers)

### GAME INTEGRATION:
- Loading screen image
- Poster visible on walls in rear areas (Bar-le-Duc, Paris levels)
- Collectible: Player can examine poster, read text
- Ironic contrast: Poster's triumphalism vs. player's experience of horror
- NPC dialogue: "Have you seen the posters? 'We'll get them!' If only it were that easy."

## POSTER #2: "ILS NE PASSERONT PAS!"
### ("THEY SHALL NOT PASS!")

**Date**: April 1916
**Size**: 100cm x 70cm
**Artist**: Maurice Neumont
**Print Run**: 300,000 copies

### VISUAL DESCRIPTION:
- **Central Image**:
  - Verdun fortifications (stylized Fort Douaumont)
  - French flag flying above fort (defiant)
  - Barbed wire in foreground
  - Explosions in background (German bombardment)
- **Soldier Figure** (bottom right corner):
  - French *poilu* standing guard
  - Rifle at ready
  - Face obscured (universal soldier, everyman)
  - Body language: unwavering, immovable
- **Typography**:
  - **Center (massive bold letters)**: "ILS NE PASSERONT PAS!"
  - **Bottom**: "VERDUN 1916"
  - **Attribution**: "Parole du Général Pétain" (Words of General Pétain)
- **Color Palette**:
  - Dark blues and blacks (night, determination)
  - Red highlights (flag, explosions)
  - White text (stands out against dark)

### HISTORICAL CONTEXT:
- Created after Pétain's famous order
- Distributed April-August 1916 (height of Verdun battle)
- Became most iconic French WWI slogan (rivaled only by "Liberté, Égalité, Fraternité")
- Reused in WWII Resistance against Nazis

### EMOTIONAL TONE:
- Grim determination
- Defensive pride
- Endurance as victory
- Not about conquering, but about *not breaking*

### GAME INTEGRATION:
- Mission briefing screen background
- Poster in Pétain's HQ (player sees it during cutscene)
- Graffiti version: Soldiers chalk "ILS NE PASSERONT PAS" on dugout walls
- Voice line: Officer quotes phrase before assault
- Tutorial: Poster introduces player to Verdun's strategic importance
- Thematic: Slogan reflects game's core mechanic (hold the line, survive)

## POSTER #3: "JOURNÉE DU POILU"
### ("THE SOLDIER'S DAY" — Fundraiser)

**Date**: June 25-26, 1916
**Size**: 90cm x 60cm
**Artist**: Théophile Steinlen
**Event**: National fundraising day for soldiers' welfare

### VISUAL DESCRIPTION:
- **Central Figure**:
  - Elderly French *poilu* (veteran, bearded)
  - Weathered face, exhausted but resolute
  - Holding tin cup (for donations)
  - Uniform worn, patched, muddy
  - Expression: gratitude and weariness
- **Background**:
  - Trench environment (sandbags, duckboards)
  - Other soldiers in distance (shadowy, faceless)
  - Gray, muted colors (realism, not heroism)
- **Typography**:
  - **Top**: "JOURNÉE DU POILU"
  - **Center**: "25-26 Juin 1916"
  - **Bottom**: "Aidez nos soldats!" (Help our soldiers!)
  - **Subtext**: "Dons pour le tabac, le chocolat, et les fournitures" (Donations for tobacco, chocolate, and supplies)

### HISTORICAL CONTEXT:
- "Journée du Poilu" was real annual fundraising event (started 1915)
- Raised money for soldier welfare (not official military budget)
- Civilians bought flags, pins, postcards; proceeds went to tobacco, chocolate, socks, etc.
- June 25-26, 1916 event raised 15 million francs
- Steinlen (famous artist) volunteered poster design

### EMOTIONAL TONE:
- Sympathetic, not heroic
- Emphasizes soldiers' humanity and need
- Appeals to civilian compassion
- Realistic portrayal (vs. idealized propaganda)

### GAME INTEGRATION:
- Historical context: Shows civilian-soldier connection
- Found document: Poster in civilian area, Paris level
- NPC dialogue: Soldier mentions receiving package from "Journée du Poilu"
- Inventory item: Chocolate bar labeled "Don de la Journée du Poilu"
- Cutscene: Soldier receives care package, shares chocolate with squad
- Emotional beat: Contrast between poster's gentle appeal and battlefield's brutality

## POSTER #4: "SOUSCRIVEZ!" (War Bonds)
### (SUBSCRIBE!)

**Date**: May 1916
**Size**: 120cm x 80cm
**Artist**: Sem (Georges Goursat)
**Print Run**: 250,000 copies

### VISUAL DESCRIPTION:
- **Central Image**:
  - French soldier and civilian shaking hands
  - Soldier: Armed, muddy uniform, grateful expression
  - Civilian: Well-dressed (bourgeois), holding paper (bond certificate)
  - Equal height, eye contact (partnership)
- **Background**:
  - Left side: Battlefield (smoke, ruins)
  - Right side: French village (peaceful, intact)
  - Division line between them (soldier bridges gap)
- **Typography**:
  - **Top (bold)**: "SOUSCRIVEZ À L'EMPRUNT NATIONAL!"
  - **Bottom**: "Votre argent combat pour la France" (Your money fights for France)
  - **Fine print**: "4% interest, tax-free"

### HISTORICAL CONTEXT:
- War bonds (emprunts nationaux) were critical to French war financing
- Four major bond drives during WWI raised 82 billion francs
- May 1916 drive specifically funded Verdun reinforcements
- Bonds sold to middle class, wealthy (ordinary workers couldn't afford)
- Repayment: Some bonds honored post-war, others defaulted (inflation)

### EMOTIONAL TONE:
- Civic duty
- Mutual obligation (soldier fights, civilian funds)
- Transactional patriotism ("Your money fights")
- Also: Financial incentive (4% interest)

### GAME INTEGRATION:
- Historical education: Shows how wars are financed
- Poster visible in Paris/rear area levels
- NPC dialogue: Officer cynically mentions "civilians buying bonds while we bleed"
- Class commentary: Bonds affordable only to wealthy (working-class soldiers can't invest)
- Collectible document: Player finds bond certificate on dead officer

## POSTER #5: "LES MÈRES FRANÇAISES"
### (THE FRENCH MOTHERS)

**Date**: July 1916
**Size**: 80cm x 60cm
**Artist**: Unknown (government commission)
**Theme**: Maternal sacrifice

### VISUAL DESCRIPTION:
- **Central Figure**:
  - French mother, middle-aged, dressed in black (mourning)
  - Standing before portrait of soldier (her son, deceased)
  - Expression: Grief but also pride
  - Hand on heart
  - Tricolor ribbon pinned to dress
- **Background**:
  - Simple domestic interior (working-class home)
  - Crucifix on wall
  - Window showing distant church steeple
- **Typography**:
  - **Top**: "LES MÈRES FRANÇAISES"
  - **Center**: "Elles ont donné leurs fils à la France"
  - (The French Mothers / They have given their sons to France)
  - **Bottom**: "Honneur à elles!" (Honor to them!)

### HISTORICAL CONTEXT:
- By mid-1916, hundreds of thousands of French families had lost sons
- Propaganda sought to frame loss as glorious sacrifice (not waste)
- "Gold Star Mothers" concept (US adopted similar imagery later)
- Deeply controversial postwar (many mothers resented glorification of their grief)

### EMOTIONAL TONE:
- Mournful but dignified
- Maternal sacrifice elevated to patriotic virtue
- Emotional manipulation (legitimizes ongoing casualties)
- Respects grief while encouraging continued war support

### GAME INTEGRATION:
- Emotional weight: Reminds player every death has a mother
- Poster in civilian areas, churches
- Contrast: Beaumont's mother receives his last letter (this poster's reality)
- NPC: Soldier sees poster, says "My mother will hang my portrait like that"
- Cutscene: Mother receives casualty notification, poster visible in background
- Moral complexity: Propaganda transforms grief into nationalist tool

## POSTER #6: "AUX ARMES, CITOYENS!"
### (TO ARMS, CITIZENS!)

**Date**: March 1916
**Size**: 100cm x 70cm
**Artist**: Paul-Émile Boutigny
**Theme**: Recruitment (Class of 1917-1918 conscription)

### VISUAL DESCRIPTION:
- **Central Figure**:
  - Young man (18-20 years old) in civilian clothes
  - Looking at mirror
  - In mirror: Reflection shows him in uniform, armed, heroic
  - Expression: Contemplative, moving toward decision
- **Background**:
  - French flag draped behind mirror
  - Rifle leaning against wall
  - Recruitment poster visible (poster within poster)
- **Typography**:
  - **Top**: "AUX ARMES, CITOYENS!"
  - **Bottom**: "La Patrie vous appelle!" (The Fatherland calls you!)
  - **Subtext**: "Bureaux de recrutement ouverts" (Recruitment offices open)

### HISTORICAL CONTEXT:
- By 1916, conscription was universal (all able men 18-48)
- But enforcement uneven; some evaded via medical exemptions, essential industries
- Posters aimed to shame non-volunteers and pressure borderline cases
- Phrase "Aux armes, citoyens" from *La Marseillaise* (national anthem)

### EMOTIONAL TONE:
- Aspirational (be the hero you see in the mirror)
- Peer pressure (your countrymen are fighting; will you?)
- Masculine identity tied to military service

### GAME INTEGRATION:
- Poster in Paris level (train station)
- Flashback: Player character sees poster before enlisting
- NPC: Young soldier mentions seeing poster, "I thought it would be like the picture"
- Ironic contrast: Poster's idealized soldier vs. reality (mud, fear, death)
- Collectible: Player finds poster, triggering memory/reflection

## POSTER #7: "VERDUN: LA VICTOIRE!"
### (VERDUN: VICTORY!)

**Date**: December 1916 (after French recapture of Douaumont)
**Size**: 120cm x 80cm
**Artist**: Lucien Jonas
**Theme**: Triumphalist (post-counteroffensive)

### VISUAL DESCRIPTION:
- **Central Image**:
  - French soldiers atop Fort Douaumont
  - Tricolor flag raised (recreating iconic moment, October 24)
  - Soldiers cheering, embracing
  - Ruined fort visible but flag dominates
- **Background**:
  - Dawn breaking (new day, hope)
  - Battlefield littered with debris but French victorious
  - German bodies/equipment in retreat
- **Typography**:
  - **Top (huge letters)**: "VERDUN: LA VICTOIRE!"
  - **Bottom**: "Les forts sont repris! La France a tenu!" (The forts are retaken! France has held!)
  - **Date**: "Octobre-Décembre 1916"

### HISTORICAL CONTEXT:
- Created after October 24 recapture of Douaumont, distributed December
- Celebrated end of Verdun battle (though fighting continued sporadically into 1917)
- Framed Verdun as clear-cut victory (vs. Pyrrhic reality: 700,000 casualties, front line barely moved)
- Boosted morale entering 1917 (which would bring mutinies and disillusionment)

### EMOTIONAL TONE:
- Triumphant relief
- Vindication of sacrifice
- National pride
- Closure (even though war continues)

### GAME INTEGRATION:
- Endgame poster: Appears after player completes Verdun campaign
- Juxtaposition: Player has experienced 303 days of hell, poster declares "Victory!"
- NPC: Veteran cynically says, "Victory? We're right back where we started, and half of us are dead"
- Moral ambiguity: Is it victory if you lose 377,000 men to regain ground you already held?
- Poster in post-battle cutscene: Camera lingers on poster, then cuts to cemetery
- Game's final question: What is victory? Is survival victory? Is endurance victory?

## ARTISTIC TECHNIQUES (Common to All)

1. **Bold Typography**: Large, legible from distance (public spaces)
2. **Limited Color Palette**: Red (blood, passion), Blue (France), White (purity), Black (seriousness)
3. **Symbolic Imagery**: Flags, rifles, forts (recognizable symbols)
4. **Human Figures**: Soldiers as archetypes (universal, not individuals)
5. **Emotional Manipulation**: Appeals to duty, guilt, pride, fear of shame
6. **Simplicity**: Clear message, no ambiguity (propaganda requires clarity)

## GAME DESIGN NOTES

### How to Implement:
1. **Visual Assets**: Create posters as 2D art assets (artists use these descriptions)
2. **Placement**: Posters appear in:
   - Train stations (Paris, Bar-le-Duc)
   - Civilian areas (brief city levels)
   - HQ buildings (offices)
   - Dugouts (soldiers pin them as decorations)
3. **Interaction**: Player can examine posters closely (read full text, see details)
4. **Collectibles**: Finding all 7 posters unlocks "Propaganda" codex entry
5. **Contrast Mechanic**:
   - After experiencing brutal combat, player sees triumphalist poster
   - NPC reactions vary (some believe propaganda, some cynical)
6. **Cutscene Integration**: Poster visible in background during key scenes
7. **Loading Screens**: Posters used as loading screen backgrounds (with historical context)

### Thematic Function:
- **Gap Between Representation and Reality**: Posters show idealized war; game shows truth
- **Information Control**: Government shapes narrative through imagery
- **Moral Complexity**: Are posters necessary lies (maintain morale) or harmful manipulations?
- **Historical Authenticity**: Actual WWI propaganda used these exact techniques
- **Player Reflection**: Forces player to question what "victory" means
