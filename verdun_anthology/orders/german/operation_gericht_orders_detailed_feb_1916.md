# German Military Order - Operation Gericht (Judgment) - Detailed Planning

**HISTORICAL DOCUMENT - ACTUAL GERMAN OPERATION**

## Metadata
- **Operation Name**: Unternehmen Gericht (Operation Judgment)
- **Issuing Authority**: German 5th Army Command, Crown Prince Wilhelm
- **Date Issued**: February 1916 (planning began December 1915)
- **Target**: French fortress complex at Verdun
- **Historical Accuracy**: ★★★★★ (Tier 1 - Actual historical operation)

## Historical Context

Operation Gericht was the German codename for the assault on Verdun. The plan was developed by Chief of General Staff Erich von Falkenhayn, who argued that capturing Paris was unnecessary - bleeding France white at Verdun would force a strategic collapse.

**Falkenhayn's Memo (December 1915 - Historical)**:
> "The string in France has reached breaking point. A mass breakthrough - which in any case is beyond our means - is unnecessary. Within our reach there are objectives for the retention of which the French General Staff would be compelled to throw in every man they have. If they do so the forces of France will bleed to death."

Target chosen: **Verdun** - symbolic city, French would defend at all costs.

---

## Operation Gericht - Key Elements

### Strategic Objectives

**Primary Objective**: Bleed French Army white (attritional strategy)
**Secondary Objective**: Capture Verdun fortress system (symbolic/strategic)
**Tertiary Objective**: Draw Allied reserves away from other fronts

**Falkenhayn's Calculation**:
- French will defend Verdun at all costs (national pride, strategic importance)
- German artillery superiority will inflict disproportionate casualties
- Ratio: 2 French casualties for every 1 German
- Within 3-6 months, France will be unable to continue war

---

### Operational Plan

**Phase I: Preparatory Bombardment (February 21)**
- **Duration**: 9-10 hours (07:15-16:00)
- **Artillery**: 1,400 guns (all calibers from 75mm to 420mm)
- **Shells**: 2,000,000 shells (goal: total destruction)
- **Target**: French front-line defenses, artillery positions, communications

**Phase II: Infantry Assault (February 21 afternoon)**
- **Assault Units**:
  - Prussian Guards
  - VI Reserve Corps
  - XVIII Corps
- **Tactics**: "Bite and hold" (Ettappen-Ziele) - limited advances, consolidate under artillery protection
- **Target Areas**:
  - Bois des Caures (Driant's position)
  - Bois d'Haumont
  - Herbebois

**Phase III: Exploitation (February 22-28)**
- Capture Fort Douaumont (key objective)
- Push to east bank of Meuse
- Establish artillery observation points

**Phase IV: Attrition (March-December)**
- Continuous pressure
- Artillery bombardment
- Limited attacks
- Grind down French reserves

### Force Composition

**German 5th Army** (Crown Prince Wilhelm commanding):

**Artillery**:
- 542 x Heavy artillery pieces (210mm, 305mm, 420mm)
- 306 x Field guns (77mm, 105mm, 155mm)
- 152 x Mortars
- 250 x Gas projectors

**Infantry**:
- 6 x Divisions (initial assault)
- 25 x Divisions (reserves for rotation)
- ~500,000 men total

**Aviation**:
- 168 aircraft (air superiority over battlefield)
- Observation balloons

**Logistics**:
- 34 x Ammunition trains per day
- Dedicated railway lines
- Field hospitals and supply dumps

---

### Tactical Innovations

**Rolling Barrage** (Feuerwalze):
- Artillery advances in timed increments ahead of infantry
- Keeps enemy suppressed until last moment
- Requires precise coordination

**Stormtroop Tactics** (Stoßtruppen):
- Small units with grenades, flamethrowers, light machine guns
- Bypass strongpoints, infiltrate rear areas
- New warfare doctrine

**Air-Ground Coordination**:
- Aircraft spot enemy positions
- Direct artillery fire
- Prevent French aerial reconnaissance

**Gas Warfare**:
- Phosgene and chlorine shells
- Contaminate French positions
- Force gas mask use (reduces combat effectiveness)

## German Assessment - Contemporary

**Initial Success**:
✅ Bombardment destroyed much of French front line
✅ Fort Douaumont captured within 5 days
✅ French caught by surprise (insufficient reserves initially)
✅ Territorial gains achieved

**Unexpected Factors**:
❌ French resistance stiffer than expected
❌ Casualty ratios not as favorable (closer to 1:1 than 2:1)
❌ German losses higher than planned (logistics/attrition)
❌ French Noria rotation system effective (Pétain's innovation)
❌ Battle dragged on for 10 months (not 3-6)

---

## Post-War Analysis

**Strategic Failure**:
- Goal was attritional victory
- Reality: Both sides bled equally (~700,000 total casualties)
- French morale held (did not collapse)
- Battle drained German resources as much as French
- Western Front remained stalemated

**Tactical Lessons**:
- Artillery bombardment alone cannot win battles (enemy adapts)
- Defensive tactics evolved (deep dugouts, elastic defense)
- Rotation systems prevent unit collapse
- Industrial war requires vast resources (Germany lacked unlimited reserves)

**Falkenhayn's Mistake**:
- Underestimated French will to resist
- Overestimated German industrial capacity
- Created attritional battle that Germany could not afford
- Led to his replacement as Chief of Staff (August 1916)

## German Perspective - Soldier View

**From German Letters/Diaries**:

> "We were told this would be the decisive battle. We would break the French, and the war would be won by summer. But month after month we attacked, and month after month they held. Who was bleeding whom?" — Anonymous German soldier, July 1916

> "Verdun is a mill grinding both armies to dust. We feed men into the mill, and blood comes out." — German officer's letter, June 1916

> "High Command celebrates every meter of ground. But we pay for each meter with a hundred corpses. Is this victory?" — Diary entry, German soldier, October 1916

## Comparison: German Plan vs. Reality

| Element | Planned | Reality |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Duration** | 3-6 months | 10 months (303 days) |
| **Casualty Ratio** | 2:1 (French:German) | ~1.05:1 (slightly favored France) |
| **French Collapse** | Expected | Did not occur |
| **German Casualties** | Acceptable losses | ~434,000 (unsustainable) |
| **Strategic Outcome** | Force French surrender | Mutual exhaustion, stalemate |
| **Territorial Gains** | Secondary concern | Most gains reversed by December |

**Conclusion**: Operation Gericht failed to achieve its strategic goals.

## Historical Significance

**Why Operation Gericht Failed**:

1. **Underestimated Enemy**: French proved more resilient than expected
2. **Logistics**: German supply lines strained by prolonged battle
3. **Innovation**: Pétain's Noria system and Voie Sacrée kept French army supplied and rotated
4. **Equality of Suffering**: Both sides bled equally (negated attritional advantage)
5. **Moral Will**: French determination ("Ils ne passeront pas") matched German aggression

**Legacy**:
- Verdun became symbol of French resistance
- Demonstrated futility of WWI attritional warfare
- Both nations scarred deeply (physical and psychological)
- Influenced German strategy shift (later: Ludendorff offensives 1918)
