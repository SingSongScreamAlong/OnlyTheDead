# ARTILLERY DATA — VERDUN 1916

---

## GERMAN ARTILLERY

### Heavy Siege Artillery

#### **420mm "Big Bertha" Howitzer (Dicke Bertha)**
- **Manufacturer**: Krupp
- **Caliber**: 420mm (16.5 inches)
- **Shell Weight**: 820 kg (1,807 lbs)
- **Range**: 9.3 km (5.8 miles)
- **Rate of Fire**: 1 round per 8 minutes
- **Crew**: 200+ (including transport/assembly)
- **Mobility**: Railway-mounted or dismantled for transport
- **Effect**: Penetrates 3-4 meters of reinforced concrete
- **Psychological Impact**: Massive blast radius, terrifying sound
- **Verdun Use**: Limited (primarily for fort bombardment)
- **Historical Note**: Developed for Belgian fort destruction (1914)

---

#### **305mm Škoda Howitzer (Austrian)**
- **Caliber**: 305mm (12 inches)
- **Shell Weight**: 380 kg (838 lbs)
- **Range**: 12 km (7.5 miles)
- **Rate of Fire**: 1 round per 5 minutes
- **Mobility**: Portable (dismantled into components)
- **Effect**: Extreme blast, used against Fort Vaux and Douaumont
- **Crews**: Austro-Hungarian artillery detachments (loaned to Germany)

---

#### **210mm Mörser (Mortar)**
- **Shell Weight**: 113 kg (249 lbs)
- **Range**: 11 km (6.8 miles)
- **Rate of Fire**: 2 rounds/minute
- **Use**: Primary heavy bombardment weapon
- **Effect**: High-angle plunging fire, destroys trenches and dugouts

---

#### **150mm sFH 13 (schwere Feldhaubitze — Heavy Field Howitzer)**
- **Shell Weight**: 40-43 kg (88-95 lbs)
- **Range**: 8.5 km (5.3 miles)
- **Rate of Fire**: 4-5 rounds/minute
- **Mobility**: Horse-drawn (6-8 horses)
- **Use**: Standard heavy artillery, backbone of German bombardments
- **Effect**: General destruction, cratering, wire-cutting

---

### Field Artillery

#### **77mm FK 16 (Feldkanone — Field Gun)**
- **Shell Weight**: 6.8 kg (15 lbs)
- **Range**: 9 km (5.6 miles)
- **Rate of Fire**: 10-15 rounds/minute
- **Mobility**: Horse-drawn (4-6 horses)
- **Use**: Direct and indirect fire support
- **Effect**: Anti-personnel, shrapnel, suppression fire

---

### Specialized Munitions

#### **Green Cross Shells (Grünkreuz)**
- **Type**: Phosgene/diphosgene gas
- **Effect**: Penetrates gas masks (early models), causes pulmonary edema, death by asphyxiation
- **Use**: June 1916 attacks (Fleury, Thiaumont sectors)
- **Lethality**: High (20-30% of exposed troops)

#### **Blue Cross Shells (Blaukreuz)**
- **Type**: Arsenic-based irritant (diphenylchloroarsine)
- **Effect**: Forces removal of gas masks (vomiting, coughing), then followed by lethal gas
- **Tactic**: Fire Blue Cross to force mask removal, then fire Green Cross
- **Use**: Mid-1916 onward

#### **Yellow Cross Shells (Gelbkreuz)**
- **Type**: Mustard gas (introduced 1917, post-Verdun)
- **Not used at Verdun** (developed later)

---

### Barrage Tactics

#### **Trommelfeuer (Drumfire)**
- **Concept**: Continuous saturation bombardment, shells overlapping in time
- **Execution**: Multiple batteries firing in sequence to create unbroken barrage
- **Effect**: Psychological destruction, physical annihilation, communication breakdown
- **Verdun Example**: Feb 21, 1916 — 1 million shells in 12 hours across 20km front

#### **Sperrfeuer (Barrage Fire)**
- **Concept**: Curtain of fire to isolate sector and prevent reinforcements
- **Execution**: Artillery fires along predetermined line to cut off retreat/advance
- **Effect**: Traps defenders, prevents relief

## FRENCH ARTILLERY

### Heavy Artillery

#### **274mm Canon de 274 modèle 1917 Schneider (Naval Gun)**
- **Origin**: Removed from ships, mounted on railway carriages
- **Shell Weight**: 240 kg (529 lbs)
- **Range**: 30 km (18.6 miles)
- **Use**: Long-range counter-battery fire
- **Verdun**: Deployed late 1916 for counteroffensive

---

#### **220mm Canon de 220 L mle 1917 (Long Gun)**
- **Shell Weight**: 108 kg (238 lbs)
- **Range**: 22 km (13.7 miles)
- **Use**: Counter-battery, fort bombardment

---

#### **155mm GPF (Grande Puissance Filloux)**
- **Shell Weight**: 43 kg (95 lbs)
- **Range**: 16.2 km (10 miles)
- **Rate of Fire**: 2 rounds/minute
- **Mobility**: Towed (motorized or horse)
- **Use**: Mainstay of French heavy artillery
- **Effect**: General bombardment, counter-battery
- **Verdun**: Extensively used, Nivelle relied on massed 155mm batteries

---

### Field Artillery

#### **75mm Canon de 75 modèle 1897 (Soixante-Quinze)**
- **Revolutionary Design**: Hydro-pneumatic recoil system (gun barrel recoils, carriage remains stable)
- **Shell Weight**: 5.3-7.25 kg (12-16 lbs, depending on shell type)
- **Range**: 8.5 km (5.3 miles)
- **Rate of Fire**: 15-20 rounds/minute (rapid fire), sustained 6-8 rounds/minute
- **Mobility**: Horse-drawn (6 horses standard)
- **Crew**: 6 men
- **Ammunition Types**:
  - **Obus explosif** (High Explosive)
  - **Obus à balles** (Shrapnel) — 290 lead balls + explosive charge
  - **Obus fumigène** (Smoke)
  - **Obus éclairant** (Illumination)
- **Use**: Backbone of French artillery, used for direct and indirect fire
- **Effectiveness**: Dominated European artillery 1900-1914, still effective WWI
- **Verdun**: ~1,000+ deployed by mid-1916
- **Cultural Significance**: "Le Canon de Victoire" (Victory Gun), symbol of French military innovation

---

### Trench Mortars

#### **Mortier de 58mm type 2 (Crapouillot)**
- **Nickname**: "Little Toad" (crapouillot)
- **Shell Weight**: 1.5 kg (3.3 lbs)
- **Range**: 500-600 meters
- **Rate of Fire**: 15-20 rounds/minute
- **Use**: Close-range trench bombardment
- **Effect**: Suppressive fire, bunker destruction

---

### Barrage Tactics (French)

#### **Barrage Roulant (Rolling/Creeping Barrage)**
- **Concept**: Artillery fire advances in timed increments ahead of infantry assault
- **Execution**: Pre-planned schedule, shells land 50-100m ahead of advancing troops
- **Innovation**: Coordinated with infantry advance using precise timing
- **Nivelle's Contribution**: Perfected at Verdun (Oct 1916 counteroffensive)
- **Effect**: Infantry advances under protective curtain of fire, surprises defenders
- **Risk**: If infantry too slow, barrage leaves them exposed; if too fast, hit by own shells

#### **Concentration Fire (Tir de Concentration)**
- **Concept**: Multiple batteries target single point simultaneously
- **Use**: Fort bombardment, strongpoint destruction
- **Execution**: Centralized artillery command (Pétain's innovation)
- **Effect**: Overwhelming firepower at critical point

## COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS

| Feature                          | German Artillery | French Artillery |
|----------------------------------|------------------|------------------|
| **Initial Advantage (Feb 1916)** | Superior numbers (~1,220 guns vs ~270 French) | Inferior |
| **Heaviest Guns**                | 420mm Big Bertha | 274mm Naval (introduced late 1916) |
| **Best Field Gun**               | 77mm FK 16 (good) | 75mm M1897 (superior) |
| **Rate of Fire (Field)**         | 10-15 rpm | 15-20 rpm |
| **Command Structure**            | Decentralized (Corps level) | Centralized (Pétain/Nivelle innovation) |
| **Innovation**                   | Gas shells (Green/Blue Cross) | Rolling barrage perfected |
| **Logistics**                    | Railway-dependent | La Voie Sacrée (truck supply) |
| **Doctrine**                     | Attrition through bombardment | Artillery conquers, infantry occupies (Nivelle) |

## AMMUNITION EXPENDITURE (VERDUN 1916)

### German
- **February 21 (Day 1)**: ~1,000,000 shells (first 12 hours)
- **Total (Feb-Dec 1916)**: ~22,000,000 shells (estimated)
- **Logistics**: Transported via Metz-Stenay railway

### French
- **February 21 (Day 1)**: Minimal (270 guns, limited ammunition)
- **Peak (May-Dec 1916)**: ~15,000,000 shells (estimated)
- **Logistics**: La Voie Sacrée (3,900 trucks, 190,000 tons/week)

### Combined Total
- **Estimated ~37-40 million shells fired at Verdun in 303 days**
- **Average**: ~130,000 shells/day
- **Peak days**: 200,000+ shells/day

## EFFECTS OF ARTILLERY

### Physical
- **Cratering**: Some craters 10+ meters deep, 20+ meters diameter
- **Deforestation**: Bois des Caures, Fleury, Mort-Homme forests obliterated
- **Fortification Destruction**: Forts damaged but not destroyed (concrete held)
- **Landscape Transformation**: Permanent alteration (Zone Rouge)

### Human
- **Casualties**: 60-70% of all WWI casualties caused by artillery
- **Blast Injuries**: Concussive trauma, ruptured organs, dismemberment
- **Shrapnel Wounds**: Lead/steel fragments, often infected
- **Burial Alive**: Trench collapses during bombardment
- **Shell Shock**: Psychological breakdown from sustained bombardment

### Tactical
- **Communication Breakdown**: Telephone lines severed
- **Isolation**: Units cut off from command
- **Suppression**: Infantry pinned in dugouts, unable to fight
- **Attrition**: Slow grinding down of forces

## INNOVATIONS AT VERDUN

### German
1. **Coordinated Gas Attacks**: Green Cross + Blue Cross combinations
2. **Massed Heavy Artillery**: Concentration of siege guns unprecedented
3. **Railway Guns**: Long-range bombardment from rear

### French
1. **Centralized Artillery Command**: Pétain/Nivelle system, all guns under single control
2. **Rolling Barrage Perfection**: Timed coordination with infantry assault
3. **Motorized Logistics**: La Voie Sacrée truck supply (replaced railway dependence)
4. **Rapid Reinforcement**: Rotation system (Noria) kept artillery supplied with fresh crews

## COUNTER-BATTERY FIRE

### Challenge
- **Detection**: Locate enemy guns (hidden, camouflaged)
- **Observation**: Aircraft, balloons, forward observers
- **Calculation**: Compute trajectory, windage, temperature
- **Timing**: Fire before enemy relocates

### French Advantage (Mid-1916 Onward)
- **Air Superiority**: Observation aircraft spotted German batteries
- **Flash-Spotting**: Observers triangulate muzzle flashes
- **Sound-Ranging**: Microphones detect gun reports, calculate position
- **Rapid Response**: Centralized command allowed fast counter-battery missions

## PSYCHOLOGICAL IMPACT

### "Shell Shock" (Combat Stress Reaction)
- **Symptoms**: Tremors, paralysis, mutism, catatonia, hallucinations
- **Cause**: Prolonged exposure to bombardment (fear + concussive trauma)
- **Treatment (1916)**: Primitive — rest, sedation, or accusation of cowardice
- **Prevalence**: Thousands of cases, many untreated

### Dehumanization of Warfare
- **Artillery as Impersonal Killer**: Death from unseen enemy, miles away
- **Loss of Agency**: Soldier helpless against bombardment
- **Transformation of Landscape**: Familiar terrain becomes alien, hostile
- **Sensory Overload**: Sound, smell, sight of constant destruction

---

## LEGACY

### Tactical
- **Artillery Dominance**: WWI confirmed artillery as "Queen of Battle"
- **Interwar Doctrine**: Maginot Line designed to withstand artillery (lesson learned)
- **WWII**: Artillery remained critical (Katyusha, Long Tom, etc.)

### Technological
- **Precision Fire**: Verdun innovations (rolling barrage, counter-battery) refined in WWII
- **Logistics**: Motorized supply (La Voie Sacrée) model for modern armies

### Cultural
- **Symbol of WWI**: Artillery bombardment epitomizes industrial warfare's horror
- **Literature**: "Storm of steel" (Jünger), "barrage" as metaphor for overwhelming force
- **Memory**: Verdun artillery = futility, mechanized slaughter
