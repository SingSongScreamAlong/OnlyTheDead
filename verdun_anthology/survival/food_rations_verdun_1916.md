# FOOD & RATIONS AT VERDUN 1916
## What Soldiers Ate, How Much, and How They Got It

**Purpose**: Document the complete food reality at Verdun for accurate survival gameplay mechanics.

**Sources**: French Army regulations, supply records, soldier memoirs (Genevoix, Pézard, Barthas), medical reports on malnutrition.

**Historical Tier**: ⭐ Tier 1 (Official rations documented) + ⭐⭐ Tier 2 (Reality reconstructed from accounts)

---

## OFFICIAL FRENCH INFANTRY RATION (1916)

### **Daily Allowance Per Man** (Ministry of War Regulation, 1916)

| Item | Quantity | Notes |
|------|----------|-------|
| **Bread** | 700g | *Pain de munition* (heavy dark bread) |
| **OR Hardtack** | 400g | *Biscuit de guerre* (if bread unavailable) |
| **Fresh Meat** | 300g | Beef, horse, or mutton |
| **OR Canned Meat** | 300g | *Singe* (canned beef), *corned-beef* |
| **Dried Vegetables** | 100g | Peas, beans, lentils (for soup) |
| **Wine** | 25cl (1/4 liter) | Red wine, daily issue |
| **Coffee** | 24g | Roasted chicory/barley substitute |
| **Sugar** | 32g | Granulated |
| **Salt** | 20g | For cooking/preservation |
| **Tobacco** | 50g/week | 7g daily equivalent, cigarette tobacco |

**Additional (Irregular)**:
- Cheese: 40-60g (when available, rare)
- Chocolate: 40g (rare, special occasions)
- Jam: 50g (rare)
- Rice/Pasta: 30g (substitute for vegetables)
- Cognac/Rum: 5cl (special occasions, cold weather, before attacks)

**Total Caloric Value (Official)**: ~3,200-3,500 calories/day

**Total Weight**: ~1.5kg of food/day (carried by supply detail)

## THE REALITY AT VERDUN

### **What Actually Arrived**

**Good Days** (20% of time):
- Full ration arrives intact
- Bread fresh (baked within 24 hours)
- Meat edible (not spoiled)
- Wine arrives (huge morale boost)

**Average Days** (50% of time):
- 70-80% of ration arrives
- Bread 2-3 days old (hard, moldy edges)
- Meat questionable (bulging cans, off smell)
- Wine arrives (saved ration from being disaster)
- Missing: Vegetables, sugar, extras

**Bad Days** (25% of time):
- 40-60% of ration arrives (supply convoy shelled)
- Bread 4-5 days old (rock-hard, green mold, inedible parts)
- Meat spoiled (eaten anyway or discarded)
- NO wine (morale disaster)
- Coffee/tobacco missing

**Disaster Days** (5% of time, but happened regularly during major offensives):
- No ration delivery for 24-48 hours
- Eat emergency reserve (hardtack, canned meat from personal stash)
- Hunger severe
- Thirst critical (water more important than food)

## THE FOOD ITEMS IN DETAIL

### **1. BREAD (*Pain de Munition*)**

**Official Specification**:
- 700g per man per day
- Dark bread (rye/wheat mix, coarse flour)
- Dense, heavy (filling)
- Baked in field ovens 5-15km behind lines

**Reality**:
- Often 2-5 days old when it reached front line
- Hard as a brick (soldiers couldn't bite into it)
- Moldy (green/white mold on crust; men cut off moldy parts, ate rest)
- Infested (weevils, maggots in older loaves; men picked them out or ate them—protein)

**How Soldiers Ate It**:
- **Soften in coffee/wine**: Dip bread in hot liquid until edible
- **Soak in soup**: Let it absorb broth
- **Break with rifle butt**: Smash loaf to break into pieces
- **Toast over fire**: Makes it more palatable, kills weevils

**What Soldiers Said**:
> *"The bread arrives hard as stone. We joke that it's better armor than our helmets. You can't eat it unless you soak it for an hour."*
> — Caporal Barthas, 1916

> *"Today's bread has more mold than bread. I cut away the green parts and eat the gray parts. Tomorrow I'll eat the green parts too."*
> — Anonymous soldier, Verdun diary

**Game Mechanic**:
- Bread quality meter (fresh → stale → moldy → inedible)
- Preparation required (can't eat directly; must soften/cook)
- Hunger restoration varies by quality

---

### **2. HARDTACK (*Biscuit de Guerre*)**

**Official Specification**:
- 400g per man per day (when bread unavailable)
- Hard baked flour biscuits (no yeast)
- Lasts indefinitely (doesn't spoil)
- Used when supply lines cut or mobility required

**Reality**:
- Issued frequently at Verdun (more shelf-stable than bread)
- Rock-hard (could break teeth)
- Tasteless (flour and water only)
- Often dated from 1914-1915 (years old)

**How Soldiers Ate It**:
- **Soak in coffee**: 15-20 minutes until soft enough to chew
- **Soak in soup**: Becomes edible mush
- **Grind into crumbs**: Mix with water to make paste
- **Desperate**: Gnaw on corners (risk breaking teeth)

**What Soldiers Said**:
> *"Hardtack is edible only in the technical sense. It fills your stomach but brings no joy."*
> — Maurice Genevoix, 1916

**Game Mechanic**:
- Emergency ration (lightweight, doesn't spoil)
- Requires liquid to eat (can't consume dry)
- Low morale impact (keeps you alive, but miserable)

---

### **3. MEAT**

#### **A. FRESH MEAT (Rare at Verdun)**

**Official Specification**:
- 300g beef, horse, or mutton
- Delivered on the hoof or butchered in rear

**Reality at Verdun**:
- Almost never arrived fresh at front line
- Impossible to deliver live animals under artillery fire
- When available, butchered in support line, sent forward immediately
- Spoiled quickly (no refrigeration, summer heat)

**How Cooked**:
- Boiled in *marmite* (large pot) to make stew
- Grilled over fire (risky; smoke = artillery target)
- Mixed with vegetables to make *rata* (French Army stew)

#### **B. CANNED MEAT (*Singe* / "Monkey Meat")**

**Official Specification**:
- 300g canned beef (*bœuf bouilli*) or corned beef
- French-produced or imported (Madagascar, South America)
- Sealed in tin can

**Reality**:
- Issued 80% of the time at Verdun
- Called *singe* ("monkey") by soldiers (joke: "This isn't beef, it's monkey")
- Quality ranged from acceptable to horrifying

**Quality Issues**:
- **Bulging cans**: Gas buildup from bacteria (botulism risk)
  - Official rule: Discard bulging cans
  - Reality: Men ate them anyway (hunger > safety)
  - Result: Stomach cramps, diarrhea, sometimes death
- **Rancid fat**: White/yellow fat layer on top (congealed, foul-smelling)
  - Men scraped it off, ate meat beneath
- **Gristle and bone**: More than meat sometimes
- **Mystery meat**: Often horse or unidentifiable animal

**How Soldiers Ate It**:
- **Heat in can**: Place can directly in fire, eat hot (most common)
- **Mix with soup**: Add to vegetable soup for flavor/protein
- **Cold**: Eat directly from can (desperate; tastes awful)
- **Fry**: If fat available, fry in mess tin

**What Soldiers Said**:
> *"The *singe* arrived today. We heated the cans and ate. It tasted like death but filled the stomach."*
> — Soldier letter, April 1916

> *"I opened a can. The smell made me gag. But I was too hungry not to eat. I closed my eyes and swallowed."*
> — Diary entry, Verdun 1916

**Game Mechanic**:
- Inspect can (visual cues: bulging, rusted, dented)
- Choice: Eat risky food (chance of sickness) or discard (stay hungry)
- Cooking improves safety and morale (hot food > cold food)

---

### **4. VEGETABLES (Dried Peas, Beans, Lentils)**

**Official Specification**:
- 100g dried vegetables per man per day
- For making soup (*soupe*)

**Reality**:
- Often missing (low priority in supply convoys)
- When arrived: Old, dirty, full of pebbles
- Men spent time sorting pebbles from peas (common complaint)

**How Cooked**:
- Soaked in water 1-2 hours (rehydrate)
- Boiled in *marmite* for 2-3 hours (communal pot)
- Made *soupe*: Vegetable broth with bread or hardtack

**What Soldiers Said**:
> *"Today we made soup. More pebbles than peas. We fished out the stones and ate."*
> — Anonymous, Verdun

**Game Mechanic**:
- Cooking minigame (sort pebbles, cook, distribute)
- Communal meal (share with squad, morale boost)

---

### **5. WINE**

**Official Specification**:
- 1/4 liter (25cl) per man per day
- Red wine, low quality (*pinard*)
- Issued daily (French Army unique in this)

**Reality**:
- **Critical for morale**: More important than extra food
- **Soldiers valued wine above almost anything else**
- When wine didn't arrive: Morale plummeted, grumbling, near-mutiny

**Why Wine Mattered**:
- **Dulled fear**: Mild alcohol eased terror
- **Dulled cold**: Warming effect (psychological and slight physical)
- **Dulled pain**: Helped with minor aches, toothaches, exhaustion
- **Social**: Shared drinking was bonding ritual
- **Home**: Reminded soldiers of civilian life, cafés, family meals

**Quality**:
- Cheap red wine (acidic, sour, weak)
- Transported in barrels or bottles
- Often contaminated (dust, debris in barrels)
- Sometimes mixed with water to stretch supply

**What Soldiers Said**:
> *"The *pinard* arrived. We drank and felt human again for an hour."*
> — Soldier memoir, 1916

> *"They can take the meat. They can take the bread. But if they take the wine, we'll mutiny."*
> — Attributed saying, French Army

**Game Mechanic**:
- Wine = morale boost (significant)
- Missing wine = morale penalty
- Drunkenness if consumed too fast (impairs gameplay)

---

### **6. COFFEE (Substitute)**

**Official Specification**:
- 24g roasted chicory or barley (coffee substitute)
- Real coffee unavailable (German blockade, scarcity)

**Reality**:
- Called *jus* ("juice") by soldiers (not coffee)
- Tasted bitter, burnt
- Served lukewarm (boiled in rear, cooled during delivery)
- Still valued (hot liquid in morning = small comfort)

**What Soldiers Said**:
> *"It's not coffee. It's brown water. But it's hot."*
> — Common soldier complaint

**Game Mechanic**:
- Morning coffee = slight morale/alertness boost
- Required for softening bread

---

### **7. TOBACCO**

**Official Specification**:
- 50g per week (~7g/day)
- Cigarette tobacco, loose
- Rolling papers issued separately

**Reality**:
- **Essential for morale**: Almost as important as food
- Non-smokers before war became smokers (boredom, stress, peer pressure)
- When tobacco ran out: Men scavenged cigarette butts, smoked tea leaves, dried grass

**Why Tobacco Mattered**:
- **Calming**: Nicotine eased nerves
- **Ritual**: Lighting cigarette gave soldiers something to do
- **Social**: Sharing tobacco was bonding/currency
- **Breaks**: Smoke breaks punctuated monotony

**What Soldiers Said**:
> *"I can endure hunger. I can endure cold. But without tobacco, I'd go mad."*
> — French infantry, 1916

**Game Mechanic**:
- Tobacco use = morale boost, stress reduction
- Smoking animation (short break, companionship)
- Scarcity mechanic (ration carefully or run out)

## COOKING EQUIPMENT & METHODS

### **Equipment**

**Personal**:
- **Mess tin** (*gamelle*): Two-piece aluminum tin (bowl + lid, doubles as frying pan)
- **Canteen** (*bidon*): 1 liter water bottle
- **Spoon** (fork/knife rare; spoon sufficient)

**Communal (Squad Level)**:
- ***Marmite*** (large pot): 10-15 liter pot for soup, stew, boiling water
- **Tripod**: Hang marmite over fire
- **Firewood**: Scavenged from ruins, dead trees, broken duckboards

### **Cooking Methods**

**1. Open Fire in Dugout**:
- Small fire pit in dugout corner
- Dangerous: Smoke escapes through dugout entrance = German artillery target
- Soldiers took risk anyway (hot food worth it)

**2. Trench Stove** (*poêle*):
- Improvised: Metal can with holes punched for ventilation
- Burn wood, coal, or anything flammable
- Slightly safer (less smoke)

**3. Heating Cans Directly**:
- Place canned meat directly on fire
- Open can first (pressure buildup can explode)
- Eat from hot can with spoon

**4. Communal Soup-Making**:
- One man designated cook (rotates)
- Boil water in *marmite*
- Add vegetables, meat (if any), salt
- Simmer 2-3 hours
- Distribute to squad in mess tins

**Game Mechanic**:
- Cooking minigame (gather firewood, start fire, cook without smoke detection)
- Communal cooking = morale boost (teamwork, shared meal)
- Fire risk = artillery strike if detected

## PACKAGES FROM HOME (*Colis*)

**What Arrived**:
- **Sausage** (dried, preserved): Most common, highly valued
- **Chocolate**: Rare, treasured
- **Cheese**: Hard cheese (soft cheese spoiled)
- **Bread**: Often moldy by arrival but eaten anyway
- **Alcohol**: Small bottles of brandy, wine
- **Sweets**: Candies, preserves
- **Tobacco**: Extra cigarettes or pipe tobacco

**Frequency**:
- Varied wildly by soldier's family wealth and postal luck
- Some men received packages weekly
- Others never received any (poor families, no family, postal system failures)

**Social Dynamics**:
- **Sharing**: Men shared packages with comrades (bonding, insurance—"I share with you now, you share with me later")
- **Jealousy**: Men without packages felt resentment
- **Theft**: Rare but happened

**Game Mechanic**:
- Random package arrival (morale boost)
- Share or hoard decision (affects squad relationships)

## WATER: MORE CRITICAL THAN FOOD

### **Official Allowance**:
- 1 liter per man per day (drinking water)
- Additional for cooking (shared)

### **Reality at Verdun**:

**Water Delivery System**:
- Delivered in barrels or cans from rear
- Carried forward by *corvée* (supply detail)
- Heavy (1 liter = 1 kg)
- Often spilled, contaminated, or lost to shelling

**Water Quality**:
- **Clean water** (rare): From springs or wells in rear
- **Questionable water** (common): From shell craters, contaminated streams
  - Tasted of mud, chemicals, corpses
  - Caused dysentery
  - Boiled when possible (didn't eliminate all contamination)
- **Desperate**: Men drank from puddles, licked moisture from dugout walls

**Fort Vaux Water Crisis** (June 1916):
- Garrison survived 7 days with ~2 liters per man total (not daily—TOTAL)
- Men licked walls, drank urine
- Thirst killed more than combat

**What Soldiers Said**:
> *"I can live without food for a day. I cannot live without water. At Verdun, water is gold."*
> — Soldier testimony

**Game Mechanic**:
- **Thirst meter** (more critical than hunger)
- Water quality choice: Drink dirty water (risk sickness) or stay thirsty
- Rationing mechanic (save water for later or drink now)

## HUNGER & MALNUTRITION AT VERDUN

### **Caloric Deficit**:

**Caloric Need**:
- Frontline infantry: ~4,000-4,500 calories/day
  - Hard labor (digging, carrying, fighting)
  - Cold (body burns more calories maintaining warmth)
  - Stress (increases metabolic rate)

**Caloric Reality**:
- Good days: 3,000-3,200 calories (still deficit)
- Average days: 2,400-2,800 calories (significant deficit)
- Bad days: 1,500-2,000 calories (severe deficit)
- Disaster days: 500-1,000 calories (starvation)

**Result**:
- Weight loss (soldiers lost 5-10kg during 4-7 day frontline rotation)
- Constant hunger
- Weakness, fatigue
- Slower healing, increased disease susceptibility

**Game Mechanic**:
- Hunger affects stamina, strength, morale
- Prolonged hunger = health degradation
- Weight loss visual (character becomes thinner over time)

## BLACK MARKET & SCAVENGING

### **Trading**:
- Soldiers traded rations:
  - Tobacco for bread
  - Wine for meat
  - Chocolate (from package) for favors
- Unofficial economy within platoons/companies

### **Scavenging**:
- **Dead soldiers**: Men took rations from dead (own side and enemy)
  - Not theft; practical ("He doesn't need it anymore")
- **Abandoned positions**: Searched for forgotten ration caches
- **German raids**: Capture German rations (different but edible)

**German Rations** (Captured):
- Bread (different recipe, often better quality)
- Sausage (*wurst*: dried, smoked)
- Canned meat
- Beer (rare, huge prize)
- Chocolate (better than French)

**Game Mechanic**:
- Looting fallen soldiers (moral choice, practical necessity)
- Trading interface (barter rations with NPCs)

## SPECIAL OCCASION RATIONS

### **Before Major Attack**:
- **Extra ration issued**: Full day + extra meat
- **Alcohol**: Rum or cognac (5-10cl)
  - "Dutch courage" / *gnole* ("hooch")
  - Dulled fear before going over the top
  - Some men refused (wanted clear head)

### **Christmas / New Year**:
- Special effort to deliver better rations
- December 25, 1916: Some sectors received chocolate, canned fruit, extra wine
- Often disappointed (promised feast, delivered moldy bread + extra wine)

## GAME INTEGRATION

### **Food System Mechanics**:

**Hunger Meter** (0-100):
- Decreases ~10 points/hour
- Eating restores based on quantity/quality:
  - Full ration (good quality): +60 points
  - Partial ration (poor quality): +30 points
  - Emergency ration (hardtack only): +20 points
- Below 30: Stamina penalties
- Below 10: Health degradation begins
- 0: Death (takes days, not instant)

**Food Quality System**:
- Fresh → Stale → Questionable → Spoiled → Inedible
- Quality affects:
  - Hunger restoration
  - Morale impact
  - Sickness chance
- Visual cues (color, texture, smell descriptions)

**Cooking System**:
- Gather firewood
- Start fire (risk smoke detection)
- Cook food (time investment, better results)
- Distribute to squad (social interaction)

**Ration Management**:
- Receive ration (random delivery success)
- Decide: Eat now or save for later?
- Trade with comrades
- Share with others (affects relationships)

**Survival Decisions**:
- Eat spoiled food (risk sickness) or stay hungry?
- Drink dirty water (risk dysentery) or stay thirsty?
- Share package from home (boost morale) or keep for self?
