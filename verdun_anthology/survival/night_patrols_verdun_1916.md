# NIGHT PATROLS AT VERDUN 1916
## Into No-Man's-Land: Reconnaissance, Raids, and Terror

**Purpose**: Document the reality of night patrol operations for immersive stealth/survival gameplay.

**Sources**: Soldier memoirs (Jünger, Genevoix, Pézard), tactical manuals, patrol after-action reports.

**Historical Tier**: ⭐⭐ Tier 2 (Procedures documented; specific accounts reconstructed from patterns and testimonies)

---

## OVERVIEW: WHY PATROLS HAPPENED

**Night = Active Time at Verdun**

Daylight: Too dangerous to move above ground (snipers, artillery observers see everything)

Nighttime: Only time for:
- Reconnaissance (gather intelligence on enemy positions)
- Prisoner raids (capture Germans for interrogation)
- Wire repair (fix barbed wire obstacles)
- Listening posts (detect enemy activity)
- Resupply runs
- Body recovery

**Frequency**:
- **Every night**, multiple patrols from each company
- Rotated through platoons (not same men every night, but everyone's turn came)
- Duration: 30 minutes to 4 hours depending on mission

**Casualty Rate**:
- High: 10-30% casualties on patrols (killed, wounded, missing)
- "Missing" often meant: Lost in darkness, captured, or dead in no-man's-land (body not recovered)

---

## TYPES OF PATROLS

### **1. RECONNAISSANCE PATROL** (*Patrouille de Reconnaissance*)

**Mission**:
- Observe enemy trenches
- Count enemy soldiers (estimate strength)
- Identify new defenses (machine gun nests, wire, trenches)
- Report back without engaging

**Size**: 3-6 men
- **Leader**: NCO (corporal or sergeant)
- **Scout**: 1-2 experienced men (good eyesight, steady nerves)
- **Security**: 1-2 men with rifles/grenades (protect if discovered)

**Rules of Engagement**: **Avoid combat**
- If seen: Freeze or retreat
- Only fight if cornered

**Duration**: 1-3 hours

**Success Rate**: 70% (return with intel, no casualties)

---

### **2. FIGHTING PATROL / RAID** (*Coup de Main*)

**Mission**:
- Capture prisoners (for interrogation)
- Destroy enemy position (trench raid, bomb dugout)
- Kill enemy soldiers (morale attack)

**Size**: 8-20 men
- **Leader**: Officer (lieutenant) or senior NCO
- **Assault team**: 4-10 men (grenades, clubs, knives, pistols)
- **Support team**: 2-6 men (cover retreat, suppress enemy)
- **Prisoner handlers**: 2 men (secure and escort captive)

**Rules of Engagement**: **Aggressive**
- Quick strike: Get in, achieve objective, get out (5-15 minutes maximum in enemy trench)
- Speed over stealth once contact made
- Kill quietly if possible; loud if necessary

**Duration**: 1-2 hours (including approach, raid, withdrawal)

**Success Rate**: 50% (high casualties; often failed to capture prisoner or achieved objective but took losses)

---

### **3. WIRE REPAIR PATROL** (*Patrouille de Réparation*)

**Mission**:
- Repair barbed wire obstacles in no-man's-land
- Lay new wire
- Check for German wire-cutting (sign of pending attack)

**Size**: 4-8 men
- **Wire team**: 2-4 men (carry wire, stakes, tools)
- **Security**: 2-4 men (watch for enemy, provide cover)

**Rules of Engagement**: **Defensive**
- Avoid combat (noise attracts attention)
- If attacked: Security team covers wire team's retreat

**Duration**: 2-4 hours (slow work; wire is loud, tangles, catches on everything)

**Success Rate**: 60% (often interrupted by enemy fire or patrols)

---

### **4. LISTENING POST** (*Poste d'Écoute*)

**Mission**:
- Sit in shell crater in no-man's-land
- Listen for enemy activity (digging, talking, movement)
- Early warning of enemy attack

**Size**: 2-3 men
- **Listeners**: 2 men (lie in crater, listen)
- **Runner**: 1 man (sometimes; carries message back if urgent intel)

**Rules of Engagement**: **Passive**
- Do NOT engage
- Observe and listen only
- If discovered: Retreat immediately

**Duration**: 2-6 hours (entire night sometimes)

**Success Rate**: 80% (low action, but boring and terrifying; high stress, low casualty)

## PATROL PREPARATION

### **SELECTION**

**How Men Were Chosen**:
- **Rotation**: Platoon roster (everyone's turn eventually)
- **Volunteers**: Sometimes asked for (rewarded with extra wine, time off)
- **Punishment**: Troublemakers assigned more patrols
- **Skill**: Best men chosen for difficult raids (scouts, grenade experts)

**Soldiers' Reaction**:
- **Fear**: Everyone afraid (no-man's-land = death)
- **Acceptance**: Part of duty; can't avoid
- **Fatalism**: "If it's my turn to die, I'll die. If not, I'll survive."

---

### **EQUIPMENT**

**What Patrol Carried** (Varies by Patrol Type):

**All Patrols**:
- **Rifle** (sometimes left behind; too long, cumbersome)
- **Pistol** (preferred; compact, quiet if needed)
- **Grenades**: 2-4 per man (Mills bombs, French F1)
- **Knife/Bayonet**: For silent killing
- **Dark clothing**: Remove shiny buttons, cover face with mud/cork (camouflage)
- **No helmet**: Reflects light; wear cloth cap or nothing
- **Blackened face**: Cork burnt, rubbed on face (reduces visibility)
- **Soft shoes**: Some men removed boots, wore socks or soft shoes (quieter)

**Reconnaissance Patrols**:
- **Wire cutters**: Cut through enemy wire
- **Compass**: Navigate in darkness (easy to get lost)
- **Watch**: Synchronize timing

**Raiding Patrols**:
- **Clubs**: Trench clubs (wooden, metal-studded; for silent killing)
- **Extra grenades**: 6-10 per man
- **Flare gun**: Signal success or call for help
- **Ropes**: Tie prisoners

**Wire Patrols**:
- **Barbed wire coils**: Heavy, awkward
- **Stakes**: Wooden or metal posts
- **Mallets**: Hammer stakes (loud; risky)
- **Pliers**: Cut and twist wire

**What They Did NOT Carry**:
- **Heavy equipment**: No backpacks, entrenching tools
- **Identification**: Removed ID discs sometimes (if captured, enemy couldn't identify unit)
- **Letters/photos**: Left behind (if killed, family items not lost in no-man's-land)

---

### **BRIEFING**

**What Officer Told Patrol** (15-30 minutes before departure):

1. **Mission**: What to do, where to go
2. **Route**: Path out and back (memorize; no maps carried usually)
3. **Timing**: Departure time, return time (late = assumed dead)
4. **Enemy**: Known German positions, sentries, patrols
5. **Password**: Challenge and response (returning patrol must give password or own sentries shoot)
6. **Signals**: Flares (red = trouble, green = success, white = retreat)
7. **Emergency Plan**: What to do if things go wrong

**Final Words**:
- "Move silently. Stay low. If you're seen, don't hesitate—kill or run. Be back by 03:00. Good luck."

## NIGHT PATROL: STEP-BY-STEP

### **PHASE 1: DEPARTURE (22:00-23:00)**

**Waiting in Trench**:
- Men huddle in trench, check equipment
- Smoke last cigarette (can't smoke in no-man's-land)
- Pray (some men; others just stare)
- Fear building (stomach churning, hands shaking)

**Climb Out**:
- Leader goes first
- One man at a time climbs parapet (slowly, silently)
- Lie flat on ground outside trench
- Wait for others
- Once all out: Begin crawl

**Sentries**:
- French sentries know patrol is out (warned beforehand)
- Still dangerous: Nervous sentry might shoot movement

**What You Feel**:
- Terror (leaving safety of trench)
- Exposure (sky above, nothing between you and enemy)
- Vulnerability (any moment could bring bullet, flare, shout)

---

### **PHASE 2: NO-MAN'S-LAND (23:00-01:00)**

**Terrain**:
- Churned mud, shell craters, barbed wire, corpses
- Uneven (trip hazards everywhere)
- Water-filled craters (fall in = drown or make noise)
- Smell: Death, rot, chemicals, mud

**Movement**:

**Crawling** (most common):
- Belly to ground
- Push with elbows/knees
- Painfully slow (100m takes 20-30 minutes)
- Clothing soaked in mud, water
- Wire tears skin, uniform

**Crouching** (if confident no observation):
- Low crouch, move slowly
- Faster than crawling (100m in 10 minutes)
- Riskier (silhouette visible if flare goes up)

**What You Hear**:
- Own breathing (sounds deafeningly loud to you)
- Rats (everywhere; scuttling over corpses, trash)
- Distant guns (constant background rumble)
- German voices (if near their trench; terrifying)
- Comrades behind you (rustling, breathing)

**What You See** (Very Little):
- Darkness (moon sometimes; clouds often)
- Shapes (craters, wire, corpses)
- Flares (suddenly illuminate everything)

**FLARES** (Constant Threat):

**What Happens**:
- Germans fire flares periodically (white light, burns 30-60 seconds, floats down on parachute)
- Lights up no-man's-land like daylight

**Survival**:
1. **FREEZE IMMEDIATELY** when flare goes up
   - Don't move a muscle
   - Don't look up (face reflects light)
   - Press into ground
2. **Pray**: Hope Germans don't see you
3. **Wait**: Flare burns out, darkness returns
4. **Resume**: Continue crawling

**If Seen**:
- Machine gun fire (sweeps area where movement spotted)
- Rifle fire (snipers target you)
- Grenades (if close to German trench)

**Result**:
- Dead: Killed instantly
- Wounded: Lie in no-man's-land, can't call for help (gives away position), bleed out or rescued hours later
- Escape: If lucky, crawl/run back to French trench

---

### **PHASE 3: OBJECTIVE (01:00-02:00)**

#### **RECONNAISSANCE PATROL**:

**Approach German Trench**:
- Crawl to within 20-50 meters
- Lie still, observe
- Listen (count voices, languages/accents, activity level)
- Watch (sentry positions, machine gun locations, wire gaps)
- Memorize (no notes; must remember)

**Duration**: 15-45 minutes (lie in mud, watch, listen)

**Danger**:
- German sentries walking parapet
- German patrols (might bump into each other in darkness)
- Own impatience (long time lying still, cold, fear building)

**Success**:
- Gather intel, withdraw undetected

---

#### **RAIDING PATROL**:

**Approach**:
- Cut wire (quietly; wire cutters, slow snips)
- Approach German trench (crawl last 10-20m)

**Attack**:
1. **Signal**: Leader throws grenade (starts attack)
2. **Charge**: Men rush forward, jump into German trench
3. **Kill**: Grenades into dugouts, shoot/stab Germans in trench
4. **Capture**: Grab 1-2 Germans, knock unconscious or threaten into submission
5. **Withdraw**: 5-10 minutes maximum, then run

**Combat**:
- **Close-quarters**: Pistol shots, bayonet stabs, grenade blasts
- **Chaos**: Darkness, smoke, screaming, confusion
- **Violence**: Brutal, fast, desperate

**Prisoner Handling**:
- Germans fight or surrender (varies)
- If resist: Kill
- If surrender: Drag back to French lines

**German Response**:
- Flares (illuminate area)
- Machine guns (sweep no-man's-land)
- Grenades (throw at raiders)
- Reinforcements (if raid lasts too long)

**Casualties**:
- High: 20-40% of raiding party killed or wounded

---

#### **WIRE REPAIR**:

**Work**:
- Unroll wire (heavy, tangles, catches on everything)
- Hammer stakes (LOUD; every blow echoes)
- Twist wire around stakes
- Test tension

**Danger**:
- Noise attracts German fire
- Germans sometimes wait, then ambush wire party
- Wire cuts hands (wearing gloves, but still)

**Interruption**:
- German machine gun opens fire (noise gave you away)
- Patrol scatters, crawls back to French trench
- Wire often left half-finished

---

#### **LISTENING POST**:

**Activity**:
- Lie in shell crater (2-3 men)
- Listen (ears strain for hours)
- Whisper reports to each other

**What You Hear** (If Germans Active):
- Digging (preparing trenches or tunnels)
- Talking (German voices; can't understand but note number)
- Equipment (clanking, wire cutting)
- Movement (footsteps, patrols)

**What You Do**:
- Remember everything
- Don't fall asleep (deadly; partner kicks you awake)
- Stay silent

**Duration**: Hours (2-6 hours sometimes)

**Danger**:
- German patrol stumbles upon you (fight or run)
- Artillery (shell lands in crater = death)
- Cold (hypothermia risk; can't move to stay warm)

---

### **PHASE 4: WITHDRAWAL (02:00-03:00)**

**Return**:
- Reverse route (crawl back toward French lines)
- Faster (urgency to reach safety)
- Still dangerous (Germans alert after raid, flares continue)

**Challenge**:
- Reach French wire (own barbed wire)
- Call out password: "Who goes there?"
- Sentry challenges: "Halt! Password!"
- Respond correctly or sentry shoots

**Password System**:
- Changed daily
- Example: Challenge = "Marseille!" / Response = "Lyon!"
- If wrong: Shot

**Injured**:
- Carry wounded (slow, exhausting)
- Sometimes leave dead (recover later)
- Wounded scream (give away position; sometimes gagged or told to be silent)

**Return to Trench**:
- Climb back into trench
- Collapse (exhaustion, relief)
- Report to officer

---

### **PHASE 5: DEBRIEF (03:00-04:00)**

**What Officer Asks**:
- What did you see?
- How many Germans?
- Defenses?
- Activity?

**Report**:
- Verbal (write report later)
- Details: Positions, numbers, equipment, mood (if heard talking)

**Prisoner** (If Captured):
- Handed to intelligence officers
- Interrogated (separate document: interrogation procedures)

**Casualties**:
- Count: Who's missing? Who's wounded?
- Recovery: Send party to retrieve dead/wounded (if possible)
- Burial: Dead buried later

**Reward**:
- Extra wine ration (sometimes)
- Rest (next day no duties, if possible)
- Nothing (often; just expected duty)

**What You Feel**:
- Relief (survived)
- Exhaustion (physical and emotional)
- Guilt (if comrades died)
- Dread (know you'll patrol again)

## WHAT COULD GO WRONG (AND DID)

### **1. LOST IN NO-MAN'S-LAND**

**How**:
- Darkness, disorientation
- Shell craters all look the same
- Compass broken or forgotten
- Panic

**Result**:
- Wander for hours
- Stumble into German trench (captured or killed)
- Die of exposure
- Found days later (corpse in no-man's-land)

**Prevention**:
- Memorize landmarks (dead tree, specific crater)
- Follow own wire back
- Stay calm

---

### **2. AMBUSH**

**How**:
- Germans knew patrol was coming (intelligence, pattern, noise)
- Germans waited silently
- Patrol walks into kill zone

**What Happens**:
- Germans open fire (close range, automatic)
- Grenades thrown
- Patrol annihilated or routed

**Survival**:
- Scatter immediately
- Don't bunch up
- Grenades back
- Run

**Casualties**:
- Often 50-100% (ambush deadly)

---

### **3. FRIENDLY FIRE**

**How**:
- Returning patrol gives wrong password (forgot, confused, wounded)
- Sentry nervous, shoots first
- Artillery shells no-man's-land randomly (hits own patrol)

**Result**:
- Killed by own side

**Frequency**:
- Not rare (tragic but happened regularly)

**Prevention**:
- Memorize password
- Announce return loudly ("Patrol returning! Don't shoot!")
- Coordinate with artillery (inform of patrol so they don't shell)

---

### **4. WIRE ENTANGLEMENT**

**How**:
- Barbed wire everywhere
- Crawling through wire
- Wire snags uniform, skin
- Struggle makes noise
- Stuck

**What Happens**:
- Germans hear struggling
- Flare goes up
- Machine gun fires
- Patrol member dies tangled in wire

**Survival**:
- Move slowly through wire (patience)
- Cut free (wire cutters)
- Don't panic

**Horror**:
- Wounded men tangled in wire, can't escape, die slowly

## SOLDIER ACCOUNTS: NIGHT PATROL TESTIMONIES

### **Account 1: Reconnaissance Patrol (Maurice Genevoix, 1916)**

> *"We crawled into the darkness. Every sound was a German. Every shadow was death. I lay in the mud for an hour, twenty meters from their trench, listening to them talk. I didn't understand German, but I counted eight voices. We crawled back. I was covered in mud and shaking. The officer asked what I saw. I could barely speak."*

---

### **Account 2: Raiding Party (Ernst Jünger, German perspective)**

> *"We heard them coming—Frenchmen, cutting our wire. We waited. When they entered our trench, we threw grenades. The blast lit up their faces for a moment—boys, terrified. We shot them. One ran. We let him go. He'd tell his comrades what happened here."*

---

### **Account 3: Wire Repair (Anonymous French soldier, diary)**

> *"Wire repair tonight. We carried the coils into no-man's-land. Every hammer blow sounded like thunder. The Germans fired flares. We froze. When the flare died, we hammered again. After an hour, a machine gun opened up. We dropped the wire and ran. Pierre didn't make it back."*

---

### **Account 4: Lost Patrol (Testimony, Norton Cru collection)**

> *"We got turned around. Couldn't find our trench. We wandered for three hours. At dawn, we were still out there. We hid in a crater all day, Germans fifty meters away. That night, we finally found our way back. The sentry almost shot us. We were reported dead."*

## GAME INTEGRATION

### **NIGHT PATROL GAMEPLAY MECHANICS**

#### **Stealth System**:
- **Visibility Meter**: Darkness (low), flares (high), moon (medium)
- **Noise Meter**: Crawling (low), walking (medium), running (high), wire/combat (maximum)
- **Detection**: Germans react to sight and sound
  - Spotted → Alert → Search → Attack

#### **Movement**:
- **Crawl**: Slow, silent, low profile (WASD + crouch, prone)
- **Crouch-Walk**: Medium speed, medium noise, medium profile
- **Run**: Fast, loud, high profile (emergency only)
- **Freeze**: On flare (button hold, character freezes mid-motion)

#### **Flare Mechanic**:
- **Random flares**: Germans fire periodically
- **Freeze immediately** (QTE: Press button within 0.5 seconds)
- **Hold still**: 30-60 seconds while flare burns
- **Movement detected**: Instant machine gun fire (death or severe damage)

#### **Wire Navigation**:
- **Wire obstacles**: Slow movement, damage if moving too fast
- **Cut wire**: Tool use (time investment, noise risk)
- **Entanglement**: Random chance if rushing (stuck, must free self)

#### **Objective Types** (Mission Variety):

1. **Recon**: Reach observation point, stay X minutes, return
2. **Raid**: Enter German trench, capture prisoner, escape
3. **Wire Repair**: Reach wire location, repair (progress bar), return
4. **Listening Post**: Stay in crater, listen for audio cues (German activity), return

#### **Patrol Composition** (Squad AI):
- **Leader** (Player or NPC): Makes decisions
- **Squadmates**: 2-5 NPCs
  - Follow player
  - React to threats
  - Can be killed (permanent loss)

#### **Password System**:
- **Return to trench**: Must type/speak password correctly
- **Wrong password**: Warning shot, then lethal fire
- **No password**: Assume German infiltrator, shoot on sight

#### **Fear/Stress System**:
- **Stress Meter**: Increases in no-man's-land
  - Flares increase stress
  - Combat increases stress
  - Time in no-man's-land increases stress
- **High stress effects**:
  - Shaky aim
  - Heavy breathing (audible)
  - Tunnel vision
  - Increased mistakes

#### **Permadeath Option** (Hardcore Mode):
- Die on patrol = permanent death
- Save only on successful return

#### **Rewards**:
- **Intel**: Unlock enemy position information (map markers)
- **Prisoners**: Cutscene interrogation, story progression
- **Survival**: Extra rations, rest day (buff for next mission)
