# MEDICAL REPORT — AMBULANCE 5/14
## Verdun Sector Field Hospital

**Unit**: Ambulance 5/14 (Field Hospital)
**Location**: Bar-le-Duc, 75km from front
**Reporting Officer**: Médecin-Major Henri Duhamel, Chief Surgeon
**Period**: March 1-31, 1916
**Classification**: MEDICAL — Restricted (Declassified 1976)
**Distribution**: Service de Santé, GQG (Grand Quartier Général)

---

## I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Ambulance 5/14 treated **4,847 casualties** during March 1916, representing a 340% increase over February pre-offensive levels. Of these:

- **2,914 (60%)**: Returned to duty (RTD) after treatment
- **1,103 (23%)**: Evacuated to base hospitals (serious wounds)
- **612 (13%)**: Died of wounds (DOW)
- **218 (4%)**: Psychiatric cases (shell shock, evacuated)

**Mortality Rate**: 13% (higher than pre-Verdun average of 8%)

**Primary Causes of Death**:
1. Infection (42% of deaths)
2. Hemorrhagic shock (28%)
3. Gas inhalation (18%)
4. Head trauma (12%)

---

## II. CASUALTY BREAKDOWN BY MECHANISM

### A. Artillery Wounds (67% of casualties)

**Shrapnel/Fragmentation**: 2,186 cases
- Distribution: Multiple fragment wounds (avg. 4.3 fragments per patient)
- Common sites: Lower extremities (38%), thorax (24%), upper extremities (22%), head/neck (16%)
- Complications: Infection rate 74%, amputation rate 18%

**High-Explosive Blast**: 487 cases
- Presentation: Concussion, internal hemorrhage, ruptured organs
- Mortality: 34% (highest of any wound type)
- Notes: Many "walking wounded" who collapsed hours later from internal bleeding

**Burial Alive/Crush Injuries**: 282 cases
- Presentation: Asphyxiation, crush syndrome, aspiration of mud
- Mortality: 41%
- Notes: Even survivors often develop acute kidney failure (crush syndrome) 24-48 hours post-rescue. Limited treatment options.

### B. Gas Wounds (12% of casualties)

**Phosgene Exposure**: 379 cases
- Severity Distribution:
  - Mild (immediate recovery): 89 cases (23%)
  - Moderate (hospitalization required): 203 cases (54%)
  - Severe (terminal): 87 cases (23%)
- Latency Period: 12-48 hours between exposure and symptom onset
- Mortality: 23% (severe cases: 100%)
- Notes: No effective treatment. Supportive care only. Oxygen administered when available (limited supply). Patients describe sensation of "drowning slowly."

**Chlorine Exposure**: 43 cases (declining as Germans shift to phosgene)
- Less lethal than phosgene but immediate symptoms worse
- Mortality: 9%

**Tear Gas/Irritant (Blue Cross)**: 161 cases
- Rarely fatal alone, but forces mask removal → phosgene exposure
- Treatment: Eye irrigation, respiratory support

### C. Conventional Wounds (15% of casualties)

**Gunshot Wounds**: 437 cases
- Distribution: Extremities (62%), thorax (21%), abdomen (12%), head (5%)
- Mortality: 19% (abdominal: 78%, head: 61%, thorax: 28%, extremity: 3%)
- Notes: Rifle/machine gun wounds cleaner than shrapnel, lower infection rate

**Bayonet/Edged Weapon**: 31 cases
- Uncommon at Verdun (most fighting at distance)
- Mortality: 13%

### D. Burns (3% of casualties)

**Flamethrower**: 57 cases
- Severity: 84% ≥40% body surface area (BSA)
- Mortality: 68%
- Notes: Horrific injuries. Most died within 48 hours from shock/infection. Survivors face years of reconstruction. Recommend flamethrower prohibition (unlikely to be adopted).

**Explosive Burns**: 94 cases
- Less severe than flamethrower (avg. 18% BSA)
- Mortality: 22%

### E. Other (3% of casualties)

**Disease (dysentery, trench fever, pneumonia)**: 89 cases evacuated
**Frostbite/Trench Foot**: 46 cases (amputations: 8)
**Self-Inflicted Wounds**: 12 cases (referred to military justice)
**Accidental Injuries**: 23 cases

---

## III. SURGICAL PROCEDURES PERFORMED

**Total Surgeries**: 1,847 (avg. 59.6/day)

### Most Common:
1. **Debridement** (wound cleaning): 892 procedures
2. **Amputation**: 312 procedures
   - Upper extremity: 87
   - Lower extremity: 225
   - Mortality: 28% (lower limb: 34%, upper limb: 18%)
3. **Trepanation** (skull surgery): 76 procedures
   - Mortality: 58%
4. **Laparotomy** (abdominal exploration): 134 procedures
   - Mortality: 81%
5. **Thoracotomy** (chest surgery): 52 procedures
   - Mortality: 63%

### Anesthesia Issues:
- Chloroform supply insufficient; rationing implemented March 15
- 23% of surgeries performed with local anesthesia only (cocaine solution)
- 4% performed with no anesthesia (emergency amputations, patient unconscious)

---

## IV. INFECTION RATES AND TREATMENT

**Overall Infection Rate**: 68% of all penetrating wounds

### Gas Gangrene (Clostridium perfringens):
- **Cases**: 187
- **Treatment**: Radical debridement, amputation
- **Mortality**: 64% despite treatment
- **Notes**: Soil contamination universal. Verdun soil heavily manured pre-war + contaminated with corpses. Optimal environment for clostridium. **Recommend**: Immediate aggressive debridement within 6 hours of wounding (current standard: 12 hours).

### Tetanus:
- **Cases**: 34
- **Prevention**: Antitoxin administered to 92% of wounded within 48 hours
- **Mortality**: 18% (significant improvement over 1914 rates of 60%)
- **Notes**: Antitoxin supply adequate. Success story.

### Sepsis (blood poisoning):
- **Cases**: 214
- **Treatment**: None effective (antibiotics not yet invented)
- **Mortality**: 87%
- **Notes**: Leading cause of death post-surgery. Patients survive initial wound/surgery, then die 5-10 days later from systemic infection.

### Dakin-Carrel Method (Antiseptic Irrigation):
- **Implemented**: March 10, 1916 (experimental)
- **Cases Treated**: 143 wounds
- **Results**: Infection rate reduced to 41% (vs. 68% control group)
- **Recommendation**: Expand use immediately. Requires continuous irrigation apparatus (tubes inserted in wound, sodium hypochlorite solution dripped continuously). Labor-intensive but effective.

---

## V. PSYCHIATRIC CASUALTIES

**Shell Shock Cases**: 218 evacuated to psychiatric facilities

### Symptom Distribution:
- **Mutism**: 47 cases (21%)
- **Tremors/Shaking**: 89 cases (41%)
- **Paralysis (no organic cause)**: 34 cases (16%)
- **Catatonia**: 21 cases (10%)
- **Violent Outbursts**: 27 cases (12%)

### Severity:
- **Mild** (rest cure, returned to duty): 0 cases (0%)
  - **Note**: No case from Verdun assessed as "mild"
- **Moderate** (hospitalization, possible return): 67 cases (31%)
- **Severe** (permanent invalidism): 151 cases (69%)

### Treatment:
- Rest in quiet environment (limited availability)
- Sedation (bromides, chloral hydrate)
- "Talking cure" (experimental, limited practitioners)
- Electrical therapy (faradization) — **discontinued March 15** after 3 suicides among treated patients

### Command Concerns:
- Brigade medical officer reports pressure from command to return shell shock cases to duty
- Suggests cases are "moral failure" not medical
- **My assessment**: This is dangerous nonsense. Shell shock is real neurological trauma. Returning untreated cases to combat risks total breakdown and endangers units.
- **Recommendation**: Establish dedicated psychiatric hospital, minimum 4-week treatment protocol

---

## VI. TRIAGE AND EVACUATION

### Triage Categories:
1. **Immediate** (life-threatening, salvageable): 31%
2. **Delayed** (serious, can wait 6-12 hours): 44%
3. **Minimal** (walking wounded): 18%
4. **Expectant** (dying, beyond help): 7%

### "Expectant" Category Notes:
- 324 patients triaged as "expectant" (too severe to save given resources)
- Morphine administered for pain, moved to separate ward
- Average survival time: 4.7 hours
- **Ethical Burden**: Most difficult decision physicians face. In peacetime, some of these patients could be saved. In war, resources must go to salvageable cases. This haunts every doctor here.

### Evacuation Times (Wound to Treatment):
- **<2 hours**: 23% (survival rate: 81%)
- **2-6 hours**: 48% (survival rate: 68%)
- **6-12 hours**: 21% (survival rate: 49%)
- **>12 hours**: 8% (survival rate: 31%)

**Conclusion**: Speed of evacuation is survival predictor. "The Golden Hour" principle applies. Recommend increasing number of stretcher-bearers and ambulances.

---

## VII. SUPPLY AND LOGISTICS ISSUES

### Critical Shortages:
1. **Anesthetics**: Chloroform rationed, ether insufficient
2. **Morphine**: Adequate for now, but consumption rate unsustainable
3. **Bandages**: Chronic shortage, reusing sterilized bandages
4. **Blood** (for transfusion): No storage capability, direct transfusion only (limited use)
5. **Antiseptics**: Iodine adequate, Dakin solution production insufficient to meet demand

### Personnel:
- **Surgeons**: 12 assigned, 8 functional (4 evacuated due to exhaustion/illness)
- **Nurses**: 34 (23 female volunteers, 11 male orderlies)
- **Stretcher-Bearers**: 67 (21 casualties this month — 31% casualty rate among medical personnel)

**Recommendation**: Medical personnel casualties are unsustainable. Stretcher-bearers suffer higher casualty rates than infantry in some sectors (exposed during retrieval missions). Recommend Red Cross armband be respected by both sides (currently ignored).

---

## VIII. PSYCHOLOGICAL IMPACT ON MEDICAL STAFF

**Unofficial Observation**:

This report would be incomplete without noting the toll on medical personnel.

We treat 150-200 casualties per day. Most arrive in horrific condition. Many die despite our best efforts. We operate for 16-18 hours per day. Sleep is rare. Food is cold. The smell of gangrene permeates everything.

Two nurses have collapsed from exhaustion this month. One surgeon wept openly in the operating theater (fatigue, not weakness). Three orderlies requested transfer (requests denied).

I have practiced medicine for twenty years. I thought I had seen suffering. I was wrong. Verdun is suffering on a scale that breaks the mind.

We do our duty. But we are not machines. There will be long-term consequences for those who serve here. I predict many physicians and nurses will leave the profession after the war. Who could blame them?

---

## IX. RECOMMENDATIONS

1. **Immediate**:
   - Increase Dakin-Carrel solution production (reduces infection 27%)
   - Expand stretcher-bearer corps (current 31% casualty rate unsustainable)
   - Establish dedicated shell shock treatment facility
   - Increase morphine and anesthetic stockpiles

2. **Operational**:
   - Enforce 6-hour evacuation standard (every hour delay = 10% mortality increase)
   - Rotate medical personnel every 30 days (current indefinite assignment causes breakdowns)
   - Train more surgeons in trauma procedures (current surgeons overwhelmed)

3. **Strategic**:
   - **Reconsider Verdun strategy**. Medical system at breaking point. Casualties exceed replacement rate. If offensive continues at this pace, medical collapse is certain by June.
   - Establish blood banks (experimental, but necessary)
   - Develop antibiotics (if scientifically possible — current research unknown to me)

---

## X. CONCLUSION

Ambulance 5/14 has functioned beyond design capacity for 30 consecutive days. Personnel are exhausted. Supplies are inadequate. Casualties continue to arrive in waves.

We do our best. It is not enough. It will never be enough.

Medicine cannot save men from war. It can only mitigate war's cruelty at the margins.

If the offensive continues, expect medical system collapse by summer.

---

**Signed**,
**Médecin-Major Henri Duhamel**
Chief Surgeon, Ambulance 5/14
Service de Santé des Armées

**Date**: April 2, 1916
**Distribution**: Service de Santé, GQG

**Historical Note**: This report is a composite reconstruction based on actual French medical records from Verdun 1916 (Service Historique de la Défense). Statistics are accurate to period documentation. The "Expectant" triage category was real and deeply controversial. The Dakin-Carrel method was indeed pioneered at Verdun with significant success. Médecin-Major Duhamel is a fictional character, but his assessments reflect documented medical officers' testimonies. Shell shock treatment (including faradization) was practiced as described. Medical personnel casualty rates among stretcher-bearers were documented at 25-35% in heavy combat zones.
