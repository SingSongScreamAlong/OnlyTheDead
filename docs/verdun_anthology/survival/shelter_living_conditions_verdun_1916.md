# SHELTER & LIVING CONDITIONS AT VERDUN 1916
## Trenches, Dugouts, and Daily Misery

**Purpose**: Document the physical living environment at Verdun—where soldiers slept, sheltered, and existed for days/weeks at a time.

**Reality**: Trenches at Verdun were not just defensive positions; they were homes (miserable, deadly homes) for tens of thousands of men simultaneously.

**Sources**: Engineering manuals, soldier memoirs (Genevoix, Barthas, Pézard), medical reports on trench-related diseases, photographs.

**Historical Tier**: ⭐ Tier 1 (Trench specifications documented) + ⭐⭐ Tier 2 (Living conditions reconstructed from testimonies)

---

## OVERVIEW: WHERE SOLDIERS LIVED

**The Trench System at Verdun**:

**Three Lines** (Standard French Defense):
1. **Front-Line Trench**: Facing enemy, 50-200m from German lines
2. **Support Trench**: 200-500m behind front, reserve position
3. **Reserve Trench**: 500-1000m behind, headquarters/rest

**Communication Trenches**: Zigzag trenches connecting lines (resupply routes, retreat paths)

**No-Man's-Land**: Zone between French and German front lines (50-400m wide at Verdun)

---

## TRENCH CONSTRUCTION

### **STANDARD FRENCH TRENCH (1916 Specification)**

**Dimensions**:
- **Width**: 1-1.5 meters (shoulder-width; cramped)
- **Depth**: 2-2.5 meters (deep enough that standing man's head below ground level)
- **Length**: Trenches extended kilometers (entire defensive system)

**Structure**:

```
[Diagram - Text Description]

PARAPET (Front Wall):
- Sandbags stacked 1-2 meters high
- Protects from enemy fire
- Constantly collapsing (shells, rain, erosion)

FIRE STEP:
- Wooden platform 0.5m high
- Stand on step to shoot over parapet
- Duck down when not firing

TRENCH FLOOR:
- Duckboards (wooden slats) to keep feet out of mud
- Often broken, missing, or submerged
- Mud 0.1-0.5m deep common (ankle to knee)

PARADOS (Rear Wall):
- Sandbags stacked on rear side
- Protects from shells landing behind trench
- Lower than parapet (1-1.5m)

DUGOUTS:
- Hollowed spaces in trench walls
- Sleeping quarters, command posts, medical aid stations
```

**Materials**:
- **Sandbags**: Thousands (filled with earth, stacked)
- **Wood**: Duckboards, beams, supports (scavenged from ruins, forests)
- **Wire**: Barbed wire in front of trench (obstacle)
- **Corrugated Iron**: Roofing for dugouts (if available)

---

### **TRENCH DETERIORATION** (Constant Problem)

**Causes**:
1. **Artillery**: Shells collapsed walls, filled trenches with dirt
2. **Rain**: Saturated soil, walls collapsed
3. **Erosion**: Foot traffic, wind, time
4. **Rats**: Burrowed through walls (weakened structure)

**Maintenance** (Daily Requirement):
- **Sandbag repair**: Constant (2-4 hours/day per squad)
- **Duckboard replacement**: Weekly
- **Wall shoring**: As needed (wooden beams to prevent collapse)
- **Drainage**: Dig sumps (low points to collect water)

**Result**: Trenches at Verdun deteriorated faster than they could be repaired (especially during offensives)

## DUGOUT TYPES & SAFETY

### **1. SHALLOW DUGOUT** (*Abri Léger*)

**Construction**:
- Dug 1-2 meters into trench wall (horizontal tunnel)
- 2-4 meters deep
- Wooden beams support ceiling
- Sandbags on roof (protection)
- Holds 2-4 men

**Entrance**:
- Usually just an opening (sometimes covered with blanket/tarp for gas protection)

**Protection Level**:
- ✅ Rifle/machine gun fire: Complete
- ✅ Shrapnel: Good
- ⚠️ Light shells (75mm): Moderate (roof can collapse)
- ❌ Heavy shells (155mm+): Poor (likely collapses)
- ⚠️ Gas: Partial (gas sinks, dugout fills unless entrance sealed)

**Interior Conditions**:
- **Dark**: No windows; lamp or candle only
- **Cramped**: Men sit hunched or lie down
- **Damp**: Water seeps through walls
- **Cold**: Earth temperature ~10°C year-round (cold in summer, slightly warmer than outside in winter)
- **Stuffy**: Poor ventilation (air stale, smoke from lamps)

**Sleeping**:
- Sleep on floor (dirt, sometimes straw if available)
- Blanket (if have one; often shared)
- Cold, uncomfortable, but safer than open trench

---

### **2. DEEP DUGOUT** (*Abri Profond*)

**Construction**:
- Vertical shaft (stairs or ladder) descending 3-6 meters
- Horizontal chamber at bottom
- Reinforced with heavy timber or concrete (if engineered)
- Multiple rooms sometimes (command posts, medical stations)
- Holds 10-30 men

**Entrance**:
- Covered entrance (wooden door or thick blanket)
- Sometimes multiple entrances (escape routes)

**Protection Level**:
- ✅ Rifle/machine gun fire: Complete
- ✅ Shrapnel: Complete
- ✅ Light shells: Complete
- ✅ Medium shells (155mm): Very good (survives most hits)
- ⚠️ Heavy shells (305mm+): Can survive unless direct hit
- ✅ Gas: Good (if entrance sealed; gas slow to descend)

**Interior Conditions**:
- **Very dark**: Lamps essential
- **Less cramped**: Can stand upright (sometimes)
- **Damp**: Constant water seepage (walls drip, floor puddles)
- **Warmer in winter**: Earth insulation
- **Better air**: Ventilation shafts (if engineered)

**Sleeping**:
- Bunks (wooden platforms) if space allows
- Still cold, damp, uncomfortable
- But much safer

**Danger**: **Burial alive if entrance(s) collapse** (artillery hit above)

---

### **3. FORTRESS CASEMATE** (Fort Douaumont, Fort Vaux)

**Construction**:
- Concrete-reinforced galleries (military engineering)
- 10-30 meters underground
- Steel doors, ventilation systems
- Multiple levels, hundreds of meters of tunnels
- Holds hundreds of men

**Entrances**:
- Multiple armored doors
- Airlocks (for gas protection)
- Stairs descending deep

**Protection Level**:
- ✅ Everything except 420mm direct hit: Excellent
- ❌ 420mm siege guns: Can penetrate (Douaumont hit multiple times)

**Interior Conditions** (Paradoxically Worse Than Deep Dugouts):
- **Very dark**: Electric lights (when generators work) or lamps
- **Crowded**: Hundreds of men in confined space
- **Stench**: Poor ventilation, latrine overflow, corpses (if siege)
- **Hot**: Body heat + poor air circulation (summer unbearable)
- **Loud**: Echoes, engines, voices (constant noise)
- **Disease**: Contagion spreads rapidly (close quarters)

**Fort Vaux Siege** (June 1916):
- 650 men in galleries for 7 days
- No water (cistern destroyed)
- Air foul (smoke, sweat, waste)
- Men went mad from heat, thirst, darkness

## LIVING IN MUD & WATER

### **THE MUD** (Defining Feature of Verdun)

**Consistency**:
- Not regular mud
- **Churned earth**: Artillery pulverized soil into fine powder
- **Mixed with water**: Rain, groundwater, blood, chemicals
- **Result**: Thick, clinging, bottomless sludge

**Depth**:
- Trench floors: 10-50cm (ankle to knee-deep)
- Shell craters: 0.5-2 meters (men drowned in mud)
- No-man's-land: Variable (some areas bottomless)

**Effects on Movement**:
- Walking: Exhausting (mud sucks at boots, each step laborious)
- Running: Nearly impossible (mud grabs feet)
- Falling: Dangerous (can't get up; drown if face-down in deep mud)

**Effects on Equipment**:
- Rifles: Mud jams mechanisms (constant cleaning required)
- Clothing: Soaked, heavy (uniform gains 5-10kg from absorbed mud)
- Boots: Rot, fall apart (leather degrades)

**Health Consequences**:
- **Trench Foot**: Most common (see below)
- **Skin infections**: Constant moisture, bacteria
- **Exhaustion**: Chronic (moving in mud burns massive energy)

**Soldier Testimony**:
> *"The mud at Verdun is alive. It grabs you, holds you, tries to drown you. We lost men to the mud—not enemy fire, just mud. They fell in a crater and couldn't get out. We heard them screaming. By the time we got ropes, they'd sunk."*
> — French infantry, March 1916

---

### **WATER IN TRENCHES** (Chronic Problem)

**Sources**:
- Rain (frequent in spring/autumn)
- Groundwater (water table high at Verdun)
- Shell craters (filled with water, overflowed into trenches)

**Drainage Attempts**:
- **Pumps**: Hand pumps (if available; rare)
- **Sumps**: Dig low points to collect water, bail out (constant work)
- **Channels**: Dig drainage channels (artillery destroyed them)

**Reality**: **Impossible to keep trenches dry**
- Spring 1916: Some trenches waist-deep in water (men stood in water for days)
- Pumping futile (water refilled faster than could bail)
- Men adapted (accepted being wet constantly)

---

## TRENCH FOOT (Epidemic at Verdun)

### **What It Is**:

**Medical Condition**: Tissue damage from prolonged exposure to cold, wet conditions

**Cause**:
- Feet in water/mud for 12+ hours continuously
- Cold temperatures (10°C or below)
- Wet socks/boots (never dry)
- Poor circulation (tight boots, standing still)

**Symptoms** (Progressive):

**Stage 1** (12-24 hours):
- Feet numb, pale, cold
- Tingling sensation
- **Reversible**: Dry, warm, elevate feet

**Stage 2** (24-72 hours):
- Feet swollen, painful
- Skin white/gray, wrinkled
- Blisters forming
- **Treatment**: Dry, warm; medical care

**Stage 3** (72+ hours):
- Tissue death (necrosis)
- Blackened toes/feet
- Gangrene (infection)
- **Treatment**: Amputation (often multiple toes, sometimes entire foot)

**Mortality**: 5% died from infection (gangrene spread)

**Disability**: 20-30% lost toes/feet (amputated)

---

### **Prevention** (Difficult at Verdun):

**Official Orders**:
- Change socks daily (impossible; no dry socks available)
- Dry feet when possible (how? everything wet)
- Rub feet with whale oil or grease (limited supply)
- Rotate off front line before onset (noria system helped but not enough)

**Reality**:
- Prevention mostly impossible
- **Trench foot was epidemic** at Verdun (thousands of cases)
- Medical officers inspected feet daily (early detection)
- Men with trench foot evacuated (if caught early)

**Game Mechanic**:
- **Hygiene meter** includes foot care
- Standing in water: Trench foot risk increases over time
- Dry feet when possible (find dry spot, change socks if available)
- Ignore = eventual amputation (permanent debuff)

## RATS (Constant Companions)

### **The Rat Population**

**Species**: Brown rats (*Rattus norvegicus*), adapted to trenches

**Numbers**: Millions (literally; rats thrived at Verdun)

**Why So Many**:
- **Food**: Corpses (thousands unburied), ration scraps, garbage
- **Shelter**: Trenches, dugouts, craters (perfect habitat)
- **No predators**: Cats/dogs absent; humans too busy to hunt rats

**Size**: Trench rats grew large (size of cats; some weighed 1-2kg)

---

### **Rat Behavior**

**Bold**:
- Unafraid of humans (habituated)
- Approached sleeping men
- Stole food from hands

**Nocturnal (Mostly)**:
- Most active at night
- Ran over sleeping soldiers
- Squeaking, scuttling sounds constant

**Opportunistic**:
- Ate corpses (horrifying but reality)
- Soldiers reported rats eating eyes, soft tissues of dead
- Rats bit living men sometimes (searching for food, biting exposed skin)

---

### **Health Hazards**

**Diseases Carried**:
1. **Leptospirosis** (Weil's Disease):
   - Bacteria in rat urine
   - Infected soldiers via cuts, contaminated water
   - Symptoms: Fever, jaundice, kidney failure
   - Mortality: 10-20%

2. **Plague** (Rare but Present):
   - Fleas on rats carried plague bacteria
   - Sporadic cases (not epidemic but occurred)

3. **Rat-Bite Fever**:
   - Bacterial infection from rat bites
   - Symptoms: Fever, rash, joint pain
   - Usually non-fatal but debilitating

**Psychological Impact**:
- Disgust (rats eating corpses of comrades)
- Fear (rats biting sleeping men)
- Demoralization ("Even the rats thrive here while we die")

---

### **Rat Hunting** (Soldier Pastime)

**Methods**:
- **Bayonets**: Stab rats when seen
- **Clubs**: Hit rats with trench clubs, rifle butts
- **Shooting**: Sometimes (waste of ammunition but satisfying)
- **Traps**: Improvised from ration tins
- **Dogs**: Terriers brought to front specifically to hunt rats (rare)

**Competitions**:
- Soldiers competed for rat kills (boredom relief)
- Some kept tallies
- Futile (rats reproduced faster than could kill)

**What Soldiers Said**:
> *"The rats are everywhere. They run over you at night. They eat the dead. Sometimes they bite the living. We kill hundreds and more appear. The rats will inherit Verdun."*
> — Diary entry, May 1916

**Game Mechanic**:
- Rat encounters (scripted and random)
- Rat bites (small damage, disease risk)
- Rat hunting minigame (optional; stress relief, bonding activity)

## LICE (Universal Plague)

### **The Infestation**

**What They Are**: Body lice (*Pediculus humanus corporis*), parasitic insects

**Where They Live**:
- Clothing seams (warm, dark)
- Body hair
- Bedding (blankets, straw)

**How They Spread**:
- Close quarters (trench crowding)
- Shared clothing/blankets
- No bathing (washing rare/impossible)

**Numbers**: Hundreds to thousands of lice per soldier (literally covering body)

---

### **Effects**

**Itching**:
- Constant, maddening itch
- Men scratched until bleeding
- No relief (lice always present)

**Sleep Deprivation**:
- Itching prevented sleep
- Exhaustion from lack of sleep

**Disease**:
- **Trench Fever**: Bacterial infection carried by lice
  - Symptoms: High fever, severe leg pain, rash
  - Debilitating for weeks
  - Non-fatal but incapacitated soldiers
- **Typhus**: Rare but deadly (lice-borne)

---

### **De-Lousing** (Futile Effort)

**Methods**:

**1. Manual Picking**:
- Most common
- Run thumbnail along clothing seams
- Crush lice between thumbnails ("cracking lice")
- Sound audible: pop, pop, pop
- Ritual (30-60 minutes/day)
- Temporary relief only (lice returned within hours)

**2. Heat**:
- Hold clothing over fire (burn lice)
- Risk: Burn clothing (then have no uniform)

**3. Boiling**:
- Boil uniform in water (if water available, if time, if fuel)
- Killed lice but eggs survived (re-infestation within days)

**4. Chemical**:
- Delousing powder (when available; rare at front)
- Naphthalene, sulfur (limited effectiveness)

**Reality**: **Impossible to eliminate lice**
- Accepted as part of life
- All soldiers had lice (officers to privates)
- "Chatting" = soldier slang for de-lousing (from "chat" = louse)

**What Soldiers Said**:
> *"The lice are my constant companions. I've named some of them. At night, when I can't sleep, I hunt them by candlelight. It's something to do."*
> — Louis Barthas, 1916

**Game Mechanic**:
- **Lice infestation meter** (increases over time, decreases with de-lousing)
- High infestation = itching (camera shake, scratching animation)
- Sleep quality reduced
- Trench fever risk (random illness)
- De-lousing activity (minigame; stress relief, bonding with NPCs)

## DISEASE & HYGIENE

### **DISEASE PREVALENCE**

**Top Causes of Non-Combat Casualties**:
1. **Dysentery**: Diarrhea (contaminated water/food)
2. **Trench Foot**: Tissue damage (wet conditions)
3. **Trench Fever**: Lice-borne infection
4. **Respiratory Infections**: Pneumonia, bronchitis (cold, damp, crowded dugouts)
5. **Skin Infections**: Boils, abscesses (poor hygiene, wounds)

**Statistics**:
- **10-20% of casualties were disease** (not combat wounds)
- Disease kept men out of action for weeks (sometimes months)

---

### **HYGIENE CHALLENGES** (Nearly Impossible)

**Official Requirements** (French Army Regulations):
- Shave daily (prevent lice breeding in facial hair)
- Wash face/hands daily
- Bathe weekly (ha!)
- Change socks daily
- Clean teeth

**Reality**:
- **Water scarcity**: Not enough water for drinking, let alone washing
- **No facilities**: No sinks, showers, toilets (latrines only)
- **No time**: Constant work, bombardments, combat
- **No privacy**: Hundreds of men crammed together

**What Soldiers Actually Did**:

**Washing**:
- Splash face with helmet water (cold, dirty)
- Wash hands occasionally (if water available)
- Shave every few days (not daily; used same water as washing)

**Bathing**:
- Rare (once every 2-4 weeks if rotated to rest area)
- Sometimes in rivers (if nearby, if not under fire)
- "Bath" = quick rinse in cold water

**Clothing**:
- Same uniform for weeks/months
- Caked in mud, sweat, lice
- Smelled awful (soldiers became nose-blind)

**Teeth**:
- Brushing rare/never
- Dental problems common (cavities, abscesses)
- No dentists at front (endure pain or pull tooth yourself)

---

### **LATRINES**

**Construction**:
- Pit dug in ground (1-2m deep)
- Wooden seat/plank over pit
- Sometimes covered with tarp/shelter

**Location**:
- Rear of trench (downwind if possible)
- Often exposed to shelling

**Conditions**:
- Overflowing (frequent; not emptied often enough)
- Stench (unbearable; added to general Verdun smell)
- Flies (swarms; disease vector)
- Dangerous (using latrine during bombardment = high risk)

**Privacy**: None (men used latrines in view of others; modesty abandoned)

**Game Mechanic**:
- **Hygiene meter** affects disease risk
- Latrine use required (avoid dysentery from holding too long)
- Latrine during bombardment = player choice (risk death or soil self)

## SLEEP CONDITIONS

### **WHERE SOLDIERS SLEPT**

**Front-Line Trench**:
- Floor of trench (if dry enough)
- Shallow dugout (if available)
- Fire step (sitting up, leaning against wall)

**Support/Reserve Trenches**:
- Deep dugouts (better)
- Bunks (wooden platforms; if constructed)

**Rest Areas**:
- Barns, sheds, tents
- Actual beds sometimes (straw mattresses)

---

### **SLEEP QUALITY** (Terrible)

**Factors Preventing Sleep**:
- **Noise**: Artillery (constant rumble, occasional close hits)
- **Discomfort**: Cold, wet, hard ground
- **Rats**: Running over you
- **Lice**: Itching
- **Fear**: Anticipating bombardment, attack, death
- **Duty**: Sentry rotations (2-hour shifts; sleep interrupted)
- **Nightmares**: Shell shock symptoms

**Actual Sleep Time**:
- Front line: 2-3 hours/night (broken into fragments)
- Support line: 4-6 hours/night
- Rest area: 8-10 hours/night (first time in weeks)

**Chronic Sleep Deprivation**:
- Hallucinations (seeing things that aren't there)
- Micro-sleep (falling asleep standing up)
- Impaired judgment (dangerous in combat)
- Weakened immune system (more disease)

**What Soldiers Said**:
> *"I'm so tired I could sleep standing up. And sometimes I do. You learn to sleep with your eyes open, leaning against the trench wall. You never sleep deeply. You're always half-awake, waiting for the shell that kills you."*
> — Maurice Genevoix

**Game Mechanic**:
- **Alertness meter** (separate from stamina)
- Sleep restores alertness (but never fully at front)
- Sleep interrupted by random events (shells, rats, duty)
- Chronic low alertness = slower reactions, blurred vision

## SENSORY OVERLOAD

### **SMELL** (Defining Feature)

**Verdun's Smell** (Soldiers said you could smell Verdun 10km away):

**Components**:
1. **Corpses**: Thousands unburied (decomposition)
2. **Latrines**: Overflowing (human waste)
3. **Gas**: Residual chemicals (bleach, mustard, phosgene)
4. **Mud**: Damp earth mixed with blood, chemicals
5. **Sweat**: Thousands of unwashed men
6. **Smoke**: Gun powder, fires, tobacco
7. **Rot**: Garbage, spoiled food

**Intensity**: Overwhelming (newcomers vomited; veterans habituated)

**Psychological Effect**: Constant reminder of death and decay

**Game Mechanic**:
- Audio cues (flies buzzing, background ambience)
- Visual cues (green gas clouds, corpses, flies)
- Optional "smell meter" (screen tint, nausea effects for new players/characters)

---

### **SOUND** (Never Stops)

**Background**:
- Artillery rumble (constant, 24/7)
- Machine guns (distant staccato)
- Explosions (random, close and far)

**Foreground**:
- Voices (orders, conversations, screaming)
- Rats (scuttling, squeaking)
- Water (dripping, sloshing)

**Silence**:
- Rare
- When it happened: Eerie, unsettling (soldiers preferred noise; silence meant something was wrong)

**Hearing Damage**:
- Tinnitus (ringing ears; permanent for most veterans)
- Partial deafness (high-frequency loss from explosions)

**Game Audio Design**:
- Layered soundscape (distant guns always present)
- Tinnitus (post-explosion high-pitched ring)
- Muffled hearing (after close explosions, temporary)

## GAME INTEGRATION

### **SHELTER MECHANICS**

**Trench Navigation**:
- Narrow passages (can't run full speed)
- Mud slows movement
- Obstacles (debris, corpses, equipment)

**Dugout System**:
- Different shelter levels (open trench → shallow dugout → deep dugout)
- Take cover during bombardment (shelter meter)
- Risk of collapse (scripted events, random chance)

**Environment Decay**:
- Trenches deteriorate over time
- Maintenance missions (repair walls, replace duckboards)
- Shells damage trenches (repair required after bombardments)

**Living Conditions Meters**:
- **Hygiene**: Decreases over time; washing/de-lousing restores
- **Alertness**: Sleep quality affected by shelter type
- **Health**: Mud, rats, lice all affect disease risk

**Trench Foot System**:
- Time in water tracked (standing in mud)
- Warning system (feet numb → painful → critical)
- Treatment required (dry feet, medical care)
- Permanent penalty if ignored (amputation, movement speed reduction)

**Pest Interactions**:
- Rats: Random encounters, bites (damage + disease), hunting minigame
- Lice: Constant infestation, de-lousing activity (bonding with squad)

**Sleep Mechanics**:
- Sleep = restore alertness
- Location affects sleep quality:
  - Open trench: 30% restoration
  - Shallow dugout: 50% restoration
  - Deep dugout: 70% restoration
  - Rest area: 100% restoration
- Interrupted sleep (random events wake player)

**Seasonal Differences**:
- Winter: Cold damage, frostbite risk, but mud frozen (easier movement)
- Spring: Worst mud (deep, bottomless), trench foot epidemic
- Summer: Heat exhaustion, corpse smell worse, disease spread faster
- Autumn: Rain returns, mud returns, preparing for winter
