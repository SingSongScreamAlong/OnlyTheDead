# FRENCH SOLDIER'S DAILY ROUTINE AT VERDUN
## Hour-by-Hour Survival Guide (1916)

**Purpose**: Document the actual daily experience of French infantry at Verdun for game immersion and survival mechanics.

**Sources**: Soldier memoirs (Genevoix, Pézard, Laby, Norton Cru's *Témoins*), regimental logs, official records.

**Historical Tier**: ⭐⭐ Reconstruction from documented patterns and firsthand accounts

---

## CONTEXT: THE NORIA SYSTEM

French soldiers at Verdun rotated through three positions:

1. **Front Line** (4-7 days): Active combat zone, maximum danger
2. **Support Line** (4-7 days): 200-500m behind front, reserve/resupply
3. **Rest Area** (7-14 days): 5-15km behind lines, relative safety

**Critical**: No soldier spent more than 15 consecutive days in combat zone (Pétain's *noria* rotation prevented total breakdown). This routine describes **front-line** and **support-line** days.

---

## FRONT-LINE ROUTINE (February-June 1916, Active Combat)

### **04:00-04:30 — STAND-TO (Dawn Alert)**

**"L'alerte!"**

**What Happens**:
- Whistle blown by sergeant
- All men immediately to firing step
- Rifles loaded, bayonets fixed
- Scan no-man's-land for German attack

**Why**: Most attacks occur at dawn (low visibility, tired sentries). Stand-to is mandatory.

**Duration**: 30 minutes minimum; longer if suspicious activity

**What You Experience**:
- Cold (always cold at dawn, even summer)
- Exhaustion (slept maybe 2-3 hours total)
- Fear (will today be the day they attack?)
- Stand in mud, water up to ankles or knees

**Game Mechanic**:
- QTE to stay alert (fall asleep = punishment/death)
- Scan horizon for movement
- Stamina/warmth meters critical

---

### **04:30-05:30 — STAND-DOWN & MORNING ROUTINE**

**What Happens**:

**05:00 — Coffee (if available)**:
- *Jus* (coffee substitute): Chicory, roasted barley, burnt acorns
- Served lukewarm in metal cup (carried in pack)
- Tastes awful but hot liquid = morale
- 1/4 liter per man (sometimes less)

**05:15 — Weapon Cleaning**:
- Mandatory: Rifles must function (mud/dirt constant problem)
- Clean bolt, chamber, barrel with rag and oil
- Check ammunition (corrosion from moisture)
- Sharpen bayonet

**05:30 — Personal Hygiene (if possible)**:
- Shave (required when possible; lice breed in facial hair)
- Wash face/hands with helmet water (cold, often dirty)
- Check feet for trench foot (critical; can lose toes/feet)
- De-louse (pick lice from seams of uniform, crush between thumbnails)

**What You Experience**:
- Hands numb from cold water
- Lice everywhere (clothing, hair, blankets)
- Trench foot constant threat (feet white, swollen, painful)
- Hunger (coffee doesn't fill stomach)

**Game Mechanic**:
- Weapon maintenance minigame (dirty weapon = jam during combat)
- Hygiene meter (neglect = disease, trench foot)
- Lice infestation visual/audio cues (scratching, discomfort)

---

### **05:30-07:00 — BREAKFAST & RATION DISTRIBUTION**

**What Happens**:

**06:00 — Ration Arrival (if supply run succeeded)**:
- Rations delivered during night (previous night's run)
- Carried from rear by *corvée* (supply detail)
- Often shelled during delivery; casualties common

**French Infantry Daily Ration (1916, official)**:
- **Bread**: 700g (*pain de munition*, heavy dark bread) OR 400g hardtack (*biscuit*)
- **Meat**: 300g fresh meat (rarely available) OR 300g canned (*singe* = monkey meat, actually beef/horse)
- **Vegetables**: 100g dried vegetables (peas, beans, lentils) for soup
- **Wine**: 1/4 liter (sometimes 1/2 liter; French Army issued wine daily)
- **Coffee**: 24g (chicory substitute)
- **Sugar**: 32g
- **Salt**: 20g
- **Extras (irregular)**: Tobacco (50g/week), chocolate (rare), cheese (rare)

**Reality at Verdun**:
- Full rations rarely arrived (shelling, logistics breakdown)
- Bread often 2-3 days old (moldy, hard as rock)
- Canned meat often spoiled (bulging cans = botulism risk, eaten anyway)
- Wine sometimes didn't arrive (huge morale hit)

**Cooking**:
- Small fires in dugouts (dangerous; smoke = German artillery target)
- *Marmite* (large pot) for communal soup
- Heat canned meat in can over fire
- Eat bread as-is (too hard to bite; soldiers soften in coffee or wine)

**What You Experience**:
- Constant hunger (rations insufficient for exertion level)
- Eating spoiled food (stomach cramps, diarrhea common)
- Jealousy if others get packages from home (sausage, chocolate, bread)
- Gratitude for wine (dulls fear and cold)

**Game Mechanic**:
- Hunger meter (affects stamina, morale)
- Food quality affects health (spoiled food = sickness)
- Cooking minigame (balance speed vs. avoiding smoke detection)
- Sharing food with comrades = morale boost

---

### **07:00-08:00 — GERMAN MORNING BOMBARDMENT**

**What Happens**:
- Germans fire harassment barrage (routine, predictable)
- 50-200 shells on French front-line trenches
- Purpose: Disrupt routines, cause casualties, destroy trenches

**Survival**:
- Take cover in dugout
- Crouch against trench wall
- Put on helmet
- Wait

**What You Experience**:
- Earth shaking
- Deafening noise (ears ring for hours)
- Dirt/debris falling on you
- Fear trench will collapse and bury you alive
- Smell of explosives (acrid, sulfurous)

**Casualties**:
- 1-5 men killed or wounded per morning bombardment (average, varies)
- Direct hit on dugout = everyone inside dead
- Shrapnel wounds (metal fragments, flying rock)

**After Bombardment**:
- Check for casualties
- Dig out anyone buried
- Repair trench (parapets collapsed, duckboards destroyed)

**Game Mechanic**:
- Take cover QTE (too slow = injury/death)
- Random shell impacts (some predictable patterns, some random)
- Dugout integrity meter (can collapse if too damaged)
- Hearing damage (tinnitus, muffled sound after bombardment)
- Comrade casualty (must retrieve wounded, bury dead)

---

### **08:00-12:00 — TRENCH MAINTENANCE & DUTIES**

**What Happens**:
- Repair damage from bombardment
- Routine maintenance (constant; trenches deteriorate quickly)

**Specific Tasks**:

**Sandbag Repair** (2-3 hours/day):
- Fill sandbags with dirt (dirt constantly sliding into trench)
- Stack sandbags on parapet (rebuild after shelling)
- Heavy labor; exhausting

**Duckboard Repair**:
- Wooden slats on trench floor (keep feet out of mud/water)
- Constantly broken by shells or rot
- Scavenge wood from ruins, dead trees

**Wire Repair**:
- Barbed wire obstacles in no-man's-land
- Check during day with periscope; repair at night
- Dangerous (exposed to fire)

**Latrine Duty** (most hated):
- Empty latrines (bucket or pit)
- Bury waste (critical; disease prevention)
- Rotate through squad

**Ammunition Hauling**:
- Carry ammunition from support line to front line
- Rifle rounds, grenades, trench mortar shells
- Heavy; multiple trips

**Burial Detail** (constant at Verdun):
- Recover bodies from no-man's-land (when possible)
- Bury dead in rear trench or shell craters
- Wrap in tent canvas or bury as-is
- Quick burial; no ceremony

**What You Experience**:
- Physical exhaustion (digging, hauling, lifting)
- Boredom mixed with dread (mind wanders; fear creeps in)
- Disgust (handling corpses, emptying latrines)
- Danger (random shelling continues)

**Game Mechanic**:
- Labor minigames (fill sandbags, repair wire)
- Stamina management (work too hard = collapse)
- Burial detail (emotional/morale impact)
- Random artillery (any moment can be interrupted by shells)

---

### **12:00-13:00 — MIDDAY MEAL**

**What Happens**:
- Heat canned meat or make soup (if vegetables available)
- Eat bread ration (save some for evening)
- Drink wine ration (if available)

**Typical Meal**:
- Canned beef (*singe*) heated in can
- Bread (700g portion, save half for later)
- Wine (1/4 liter)
- Water (from canteen; refilled from delivery or shell crater if desperate)

**What You Experience**:
- Brief respite
- Conversation with comrades (jokes, complaints, memories of home)
- Warmth from hot food (momentary comfort)
- Knowledge that meal is over too quickly

**Game Mechanic**:
- Eating animation (restores hunger meter)
- Conversation tree with NPCs (build relationships, learn backstories)
- Sharing food option (morale boost, bonding)

---

### **13:00-17:00 — SENTRY DUTY / REST (ROTATION)**

**What Happens**:
- Squad divided: Half on sentry duty, half resting
- Rotate every 2 hours

**Sentry Duty**:
- Watch no-man's-land with periscope (stick head up = sniper death)
- Listen for sounds (wire cutting, movement, digging)
- Report suspicious activity to sergeant
- Stay awake (extremely difficult; exhaustion, boredom, monotony)

**Rest Period**:
- Sleep (try to; shelling, cold, lice make it difficult)
- Write letters home (if paper/pencil available)
- Play cards (if available)
- Clean equipment
- Stare at nothing (common; dissociation)

**What You Experience**:

**On Sentry**:
- Fight to stay awake (falling asleep = court-martial, execution if caught)
- Paranoia (every shadow = German; every sound = attack)
- Boredom so intense it's painful
- Cold or heat (depending on season)

**Resting**:
- Can't sleep (too cold, too loud, too afraid)
- Homesickness (letters from home make it worse and better)
- Companionship (talk with comrades; only thing keeping you sane)

**Game Mechanic**:
- Sentry duty: Stay-awake mechanic (alertness meter, QTEs)
- Observation minigame (spot German activity, report accurately)
- Rest: Choose activity (sleep = stamina, letters = morale, cards = bonding)
- Random events (shell lands nearby, rat runs over you, comrade tells story)

---

### **17:00-18:00 — GERMAN EVENING BOMBARDMENT**

**What Happens**:
- Repeat of morning bombardment
- Sometimes heavier (Germans targeting resupply routes before nightfall)

**Same as 07:00-08:00**: Take cover, survive, dig out casualties, repair damage.

---

### **18:00-19:00 — EVENING MEAL**

**What Happens**:
- Eat remaining bread ration
- Soup (if made earlier with dried vegetables)
- Coffee substitute (if available)
- Tobacco (if available; critical for morale)

**What You Experience**:
- Hunger not satisfied (rations insufficient)
- Darkness falling (dread; night is dangerous)
- Camaraderie (men huddle together for warmth, talk quietly)

---

### **19:00-20:00 — STAND-TO (Dusk Alert)**

**What Happens**:
- Repeat of morning stand-to
- All men to firing step
- Scan no-man's-land as darkness falls
- Germans might attack under cover of dusk

**Duration**: 30-60 minutes

**What You Experience**:
- Exhaustion (been awake since 04:00)
- Fear (darkness brings patrols, raids, uncertainty)
- Cold (temperature drops rapidly at dusk)

---

### **20:00-04:00 — NIGHT OPERATIONS & FITFUL SLEEP**

**What Happens**:

**Night is NOT rest**. Night is when most activity happens.

**20:00-24:00 — Night Work Details**:

Some men assigned:
- **Resupply Run** (*corvée*): Carry rations, ammunition, water from support line to front line (1-3km round trip, under fire)
- **Wire Repair**: Go into no-man's-land, repair barbed wire (extreme danger)
- **Patrol**: Reconnaissance, prisoner raids, or defensive patrol (see separate patrol document)
- **Listening Post**: Sit in shell crater in no-man's-land, listen for German activity

Others:
- **Sleep** (attempt): 2-hour shifts in dugout
- Never deep sleep; wake at every sound
- Rats crawl over you
- Water drips from dugout ceiling
- Cold seeps into bones

**00:00-04:00 — Late Night**:
- German harassment fire (random shells, 5-10/hour)
- Flares shot into sky (illuminate no-man's-land; freeze if caught in light)
- Machine gun sweeps (random bursts across no-man's-land)
- Sentry duty continues (2-hour rotations)

**What You Experience**:
- Exhaustion beyond description
- Micro-sleep standing up (dangerous)
- Fear every moment (flares, shells, raids)
- Cold (shivering uncontrollably)
- Longing for dawn (but dawn brings bombardment)

**Game Mechanic**:
- Night duties (different gameplay for each: resupply = stealth/hauling, wire repair = tension/exposure, patrol = see patrol document)
- Sleep interruptions (can't fully rest; stamina partially restored)
- Flare exposure (freeze mechanic; movement = machine gun fire)
- Rat interactions (annoyance, disease vector)

---

### **04:00 — CYCLE REPEATS**

Stand-to. Another day. Survive.

## SEASONAL VARIATIONS

### **WINTER (December-February)**

**Additional Challenges**:
- **Cold**: -5°C to 5°C, snow, ice
- **Frostbite**: Toes, fingers, nose (must keep moving)
- **Hypothermia**: Risk if wet + cold
- **Snow Blindness**: Sun reflecting off snow
- **Firewood Scarcity**: Desperate for warmth
- **Water Freezes**: Canteens freeze; must thaw

**Daily Changes**:
- More time devoted to staying warm (stamping feet, arm exercises)
- Fires more critical (risk worth it)
- Sleep nearly impossible (too cold)
- Rations harder to eat (bread frozen solid, must thaw)

**Game Mechanic**: Temperature meter (must manage warmth or die)

---

### **SPRING (March-May)**

**Additional Challenges**:
- **Mud**: Ankle to knee-deep, everywhere
- **Trench Collapse**: Rain saturates soil, walls collapse
- **Disease**: Standing water breeds disease
- **Trench Foot**: Epidemic levels

**Daily Changes**:
- Constant bailing water from trenches
- Duckboard repair constant
- More time cleaning feet (trench foot prevention)
- Rations spoil faster (heat + moisture)

**Game Mechanic**: Mud slows movement, trench foot risk high

---

### **SUMMER (June-August)**

**Additional Challenges**:
- **Heat**: 20°C-28°C in sun
- **Thirst**: Water scarcity worse
- **Corpses Decompose**: Unbearable stench
- **Flies**: Swarms (disease vector)
- **Sunburn**: No shade

**Daily Changes**:
- Water more critical than food
- Heat exhaustion risk (especially during labor)
- Smell overwhelming (rotting corpses, latrine, sweat)
- Soldiers strip shirts (against regulations; desperate for relief)

**Game Mechanic**: Thirst meter, heat exhaustion risk

---

### **AUTUMN (September-November)**

**Additional Challenges**:
- **Rain Returns**: Mud returns
- **Cold Sets In**: Preparing for winter
- **Shorter Days**: Less light for work

**Daily Changes**:
- Similar to spring (mud)
- Increasing cold (similar to winter prep)

## SUPPORT-LINE ROUTINE (200-500m Behind Front)

**Differences from Front-Line**:

**Less Danger**:
- Still shelled, but less frequently (10-20 shells/day vs. 200+)
- No direct combat (usually)
- Can sleep 4-6 hours/night (vs. 2-3)

**More Work**:
- Unload supplies arriving from rear
- Prepare ammunition for front line
- Cook food for front-line delivery
- Repair equipment (uniforms, boots, rifles)

**Better Conditions**:
- Deeper dugouts (safer)
- Sometimes hot food (field kitchen if available)
- Slightly cleaner (less mud, fewer corpses)

**But**:
- Still Verdun; still danger
- Random shells kill randomly
- Know you rotate back to front line in 4-7 days

---

## REST AREA ROUTINE (5-15km Behind Lines)

**Differences**:

**Relative Safety**:
- German artillery can't reach (usually)
- Can walk above ground (not in trenches)
- Sleep in barns, billets, or tents

**Activities**:
- **Delousing**: Systematic (boil uniforms, shower if available)
- **Medical**: Treat trench foot, minor wounds, disease
- **Training**: Drill, practice attacks (preparing for return)
- **Mail**: Receive letters from home, write responses
- **Recreation**: Drink wine, play cards, visit *estaminet* (tavern if nearby)

**But**:
- Know you return to front line in 7-14 days
- Can't relax; anticipatory dread
- Nightmares (shell shock symptoms)

---

## PSYCHOLOGICAL REALITY

**What Soldiers Say** (from memoirs):

> *"The worst part isn't the shelling. It's the waiting. Waiting for the shell with your name on it. Waiting for the attack that might not come. Waiting for your relief that might not arrive. Waiting to die or waiting to go mad."*
> — Composite from multiple accounts

> *"You don't think about next week. You think about the next hour. Will I be alive in one hour? That's all you can manage."*
> — French infantry, Verdun 1916

> *"After two weeks, you stop feeling. You eat without tasting. You sleep without dreaming. You kill without thinking. You're a machine that runs on fear and exhaustion."*
> — Soldier testimony, Norton Cru collection

## GAME INTEGRATION

### **Core Survival Loop**:

1. **Wake** → Survive stand-to
2. **Eat** → Manage hunger/ration scarcity
3. **Work** → Trench maintenance, duties (boredom + danger)
4. **Bombardment** → Survive artillery (random death)
5. **Sentry/Rest** → Stay awake or attempt sleep
6. **Bombardment** → Survive artillery (again)
7. **Eat** → Hunger partially satisfied
8. **Stand-to** → Survive dusk alert
9. **Night** → Work details, patrols, fitful sleep
10. **Repeat** → 4-7 days until rotation

### **Meters to Manage**:
- **Hunger** (0-100): Decreases constantly; affects stamina
- **Thirst** (0-100): Decreases faster than hunger; critical
- **Stamina** (0-100): Decreases with work; rest restores partially
- **Warmth** (0-100, seasonal): Cold/heat affects health
- **Hygiene** (0-100): Affects disease risk, trench foot
- **Morale** (0-100): Affects performance; influenced by food, letters, comrades, combat
- **Alertness** (0-100): Critical for sentry duty; sleep restores

### **Death Comes From**:
- Artillery (random, instant, unavoidable sometimes)
- Snipers (if careless, stick head up)
- Disease (trench foot, infection, dysentery)
- Exhaustion (fall asleep on sentry, court-martialed)
- Starvation/Dehydration (if supply breaks down)
- Gas (if mask fails or not quick enough)
- Raids (German patrols)
- Accidents (trench collapse, falling, misfires)

### **Victory Condition**:
**Survive until rotation.** That's it. Just live 4-7 days. Then do it again.
