# GENERAL ORDER NO. 1
## Commander, Second Army

**From**: General Philippe Pétain, Commanding Second Army
**To**: All Corps Commanders, Verdun Sector
**Date**: February 26, 1916
**Time**: 14:00 hours
**Classification**: SECRET (declassified 1968)
**Distribution**: XX Corps, I Colonial Corps, all divisional commands

---

## HEADQUARTERS, SECOND ARMY
### Souilly, February 26, 1916

### GENERAL ORDER NO. 1

Upon assuming command of Second Army this date, I issue the following directives effective immediately:

---

### I. SITUATION

The German offensive initiated February 21 has penetrated French positions to depth of 8 kilometers on right bank of Meuse. Fort Douaumont has fallen. Enemy forces are advancing toward Verdun citadel. The situation is grave but not desperate.

French resistance has been heroic but uncoordinated. Communication has broken down. Units have been cut off and destroyed piecemeal. Artillery has been insufficient and poorly coordinated.

This will change.

---

### II. MISSION

The mission of Second Army is simple and absolute:

**THE ENEMY WILL NOT PASS.**

We will hold Verdun at all costs. There will be no withdrawal. There will be no surrender. Every position will be defended to the last man.

But defense alone is not enough. We will counterattack. We will make the enemy pay in blood for every meter.

---

### III. REORGANIZATION OF DEFENSE

Effective immediately, the following measures are enacted:

#### A. ARTILLERY COMMAND (CENTRALIZED)

All artillery in Verdun sector is placed under unified command. Corps commanders will no longer control individual batteries. Artillery will be massed and directed from army level.

- **Artillery Commander**: Col. Robert Nivelle (promoted to this command)
- **Objective**: Concentration of fire, rapid response, counter-battery missions
- **Method**: Centralized fire control, telephone/telegraph coordination where possible, observation posts on all key heights

#### B. DEFENSIVE SECTORS

Right bank of Meuse divided into three defensive sectors:

1. **Sector North** (Douaumont-Vaux): XX Corps (Balfourier)
2. **Sector Center** (Souville-Tavannes): I Colonial Corps
3. **Sector South** (Verdun Citadel-Meuse): Territorial divisions

Left bank will be reinforced to prevent flanking attacks.

#### C. DIVISIONAL ROTATION (THE NORIA SYSTEM)

No division will remain in the line for more than 15 days. Fresh divisions will rotate in continuously.

- **Purpose**: Maintain morale, prevent psychological collapse
- **Method**: Staged relief, maintain continuity of defense
- **Logistics**: Coordinate with supply convoys

This is non-negotiable. We will preserve the fighting capacity of the French Army.

---

### IV. SUPPLY LINE (LA VOIE SACRÉE)

The road from Bar-le-Duc to Verdun is the lifeline of this army. It will be maintained at all costs.

- **Road Maintenance**: 8,500 territorial troops assigned full-time to road repair
- **Traffic Control**: Military police will enforce strict convoy discipline
- **Capacity**: Minimum 5,000 tons per day (target 10,000)
- **Priority**: Ammunition, food, medical supplies, reinforcements

Any officer who disrupts traffic flow will be relieved of command.

---

### V. FORTIFICATIONS

The loss of Douaumont is a disaster but not a catastrophe. Forts are strongpoints, not talismans.

All remaining forts will be garrisoned properly:

- **Fort Vaux**: Reinforce to battalion strength
- **Fort Souville**: Maintain as command post and supply depot
- **Fort Moulainville**: Rear area hospital and logistics

Trenches will be rebuilt where destroyed. Dugouts will be deepened. Wire will be replaced.

The enemy has destroyed our first line. We will build a second. And a third. And a fourth.

---

### VI. MORALE

I have read reports of panic, of units breaking, of men fleeing. This is understandable. The bombardment was unprecedented. But it will not continue.

I order all commanders:

1. **Steady the Men**: Your presence in the line is mandatory. Lead from the front.
2. **No Defeatism**: Rumors of withdrawal or evacuation are false. Anyone spreading such rumors will be court-martialed.
3. **Communication**: Keep the men informed. They are not fools. They know the situation is serious. But they must also know that we will prevail.

Tell them this:

**"Courage, mes enfants. On les aura!"** (Courage, my children. We will get them!)

---

### VII. COUNTERATTACK

Defense is necessary. But we are French. We do not merely endure. We strike back.

As soon as forces are concentrated and artillery is reorganized, we will counterattack. Objectives will be announced separately.

Every officer will begin planning limited counterattacks within his sector. I want the enemy to bleed for what he has taken.

---

### VIII. DISCIPLINE

This is war. Men will die. Units will be destroyed. Ground will be lost.

But we will not break.

Cowardice will not be tolerated. Desertion will be punished by death. Incompetence will result in immediate relief.

But I promise this: every man who does his duty will be cared for. The wounded will be evacuated. The exhausted will be rotated out. The dead will be honored.

I will not waste French lives. But I will spend them where necessary.

---

### IX. FINAL WORD

Gentlemen, the eyes of France are upon us. The Germans believe we will collapse. They believe Verdun will break our will.

They are wrong.

We will hold. Not because of grand strategy or national glory. We will hold because we are soldiers, and this is our duty.

**Ils ne passeront pas.**

(They shall not pass.)

This is my promise to France. Make it yours.

---

**Signed**,
Philippe Pétain
General of Division
Commanding Second Army

---

**Historical Note**: This order represents Pétain's immediate reorganization of Verdun's defense. While not a verbatim historical document, it synthesizes his actual orders issued February 26-27, 1916, as documented in French military archives. Key elements—centralized artillery, the Noria rotation system, and the motto "Ils ne passeront pas"—are historically accurate. The tone reflects Pétain's command style: direct, methodical, focused on logistics and morale. This order is credited with saving Verdun. Original orders are held at the Service Historique de la Défense, Vincennes.
