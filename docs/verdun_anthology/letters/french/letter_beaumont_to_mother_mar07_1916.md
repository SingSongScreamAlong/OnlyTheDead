# LETTER: Soldat Théodore Beaumont to Mother
**From**: Pvt. Théodore Beaumont, 151st Infantry Regiment
**To**: Mme. Geneviève Beaumont, 14 rue de Belleville, Paris
**Date**: March 7, 1916
**Location**: Mort-Homme Sector
**Status**: Mailed March 8, received March 15. Last letter before his death March 8.
**Censor Mark**: "PASSED / 151e RI"

---

Dearest Maman,

Please do not worry. I am well. The food is adequate and we have blankets. It is cold, but I have known colder winters in Paris.

[CENSORED — 2 lines removed]

The men in my section are good men. There is a corporal named Renard who looks after me. He is from Lille and has a daughter my age—no, younger. Seven, I think. He is kind to me, though he pretends not to be. There is also a sergeant, Dulac, who is very brave. He has been a soldier for many years and knows everything. When I am frightened, I watch him, and he is never frightened, so I try to be like him.

[CENSORED — 4 lines removed]

Maman, I must tell you something. When I enlisted, you cried and said I was too young. I said I was not too young, that I was nineteen and a man. But Maman, sometimes I do not feel like a man. Sometimes I feel like a boy who wants his mother.

I do not write this to upset you. I write it because it is true, and because if I do not tell someone, I think I will go mad. There are things here I cannot describe. Not because the censors would cut them (though they would), but because the words do not exist.

But please do not worry. I am brave. Or I try to be. And I am not alone.

I think of you every day. I think of the apartment, the smell of your cooking, the sound of the street vendors in the morning. I think of the print shop where I worked, the feeling of ink on my fingers, the weight of the type.

I think of Paris.

When this is over—and it will be over soon, everyone says so—I will come home. We will go to the café on the corner, the one with the blue awning, and I will drink coffee (real coffee, not the mud they serve here) and you will have your tea, and we will sit in the sun and watch people walk by.

I will tell you everything then. Not now. Now I must only tell you that I am safe, that I am well, that I miss you.

Do you remember when I was small and had nightmares? You would sit by my bed and sing to me. "Au clair de la lune." Do you remember?

Sometimes at night, when I cannot sleep, I sing it to myself. Quietly, so the others do not hear. It helps.

Maman, I am afraid I am not writing clearly. The candle is dim and my hands are cold and I am very tired. But I wanted you to know that I am thinking of you. That I love you. That I will see you again.

Promise me you will not cry when you read this. I am fine. Truly.

Kiss the cat for me. (Is she still alive? She must be very old now.)

I must close. We move tomorrow at dawn.

[CENSORED — 1 line removed]

All my love,
Your son,
Théodore

P.S. — If you write to me, send chocolate. Everyone here dreams of chocolate.
