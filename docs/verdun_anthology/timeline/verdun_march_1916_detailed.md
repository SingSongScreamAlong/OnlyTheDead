# VERDUN — MARCH 1916
## Detailed Timeline: Expansion to Left Bank

**Phase**: II — Expansion to Left Bank & Mort-Homme Offensive
**Duration**: March 1-31, 1916 (31 days)
**Key Battles**: Mort-Homme (Hill 295), Hill 304, Cumières, Right Bank consolidation
**Strategic Shift**: German offensive expands from right bank to both banks of Meuse
**Weather**: Early March cold/snow transitions to rain mid-month; mud becomes dominant factor
**Result**: Stalemate; massive casualties, minimal territorial gains either side

---

## MARCH 1, 1916 (Day 10 of Battle)

### Right Bank
- German pressure continues toward Fort Vaux
- French counterattacks near Douaumont fail (XX Corps)
- Casualties: ~1,200 (both sides combined, daily average)

### Left Bank
- Increased German artillery activity observed
- French intelligence reports German troop movements west of Meuse
- Preparation for left bank offensive

### Weather
- Cold, intermittent snow
- Temperature: -2°C to 4°C
- Ground: Frozen, some thaw in daylight

### Command
- Pétain consolidating Second Army command (5th day in post)
- Nivelle organizing centralized artillery command

---

## MARCH 2, 1916

### Key Event: Germans Begin Artillery Preparation (Left Bank)
- 06:00: Heavy bombardment begins on French positions west of Meuse
- Targets: Mort-Homme (Hill 295), Hill 304, Cumières, Chattancourt
- Duration: 6 hours
- German artillery: ~300 guns (addition to right bank forces)

### French Response
- French artillery counterbattery fire
- Reinforcements moved to left bank (29th Division)
- Evacuation of remaining civilians from Cumières sector

### Significance
- Marks beginning of two-bank war
- German objective: Eliminate French artillery observation on left bank heights
- French artillery on Mort-Homme threatens German supply routes to right bank

---

## MARCH 3, 1916

### Right Bank
- Limited German probes toward Vaux sector
- French consolidate defensive positions
- Noria rotation: 51st Division (decimated Feb 21-23) withdrawn, replaced by 37th Division

### Left Bank
- German bombardment continues
- French trenches on northern slopes of Mort-Homme heavily damaged
- Casualties: ~800 French (bombardment), ~200 German (counterbattery fire)

### Weather
- Light snow, visibility poor
- Temperature: 0°C to 3°C

---

## MARCH 6, 1916 (Day 15 of Battle)

### **KEY EVENT: GERMAN ASSAULT ON MORT-HOMME BEGINS**

#### 17:00 — Infantry Attack Commences
- **German Forces**: V Reserve Corps, VI Reserve Corps (~40,000 men)
- **Objective**: Capture Mort-Homme and Hill 304
- **Method**: Frontal assault following 4-hour bombardment

#### French Defense
- **Units**: 29th Division (Gen. Guyot de Salins), 67th Division
- **Strength**: ~20,000 men (outnumbered 2:1)
- **Positions**: Trenches on northern and eastern slopes of Mort-Homme

#### Battle
- Germans advance under cover of artillery barrage
- French machine guns open fire at 300 meters
- German assault troops reach French trenches, hand-to-hand combat
- Fighting continues into night

#### Result (Day 1)
- Germans gain 500 meters on northern slope
- French hold summit of Mort-Homme (elevation 295m)
- Casualties:
  - German: ~2,000
  - French: ~1,800

### Weather
- Clear, cold evening
- Temperature: 2°C to 8°C
- First clear day in week (enabled assault)

### Significance
- Mort-Homme becomes focal point of Verdun battle for next 10 weeks
- Name "Mort-Homme" (Dead Man) becomes prophetic

---

## MARCH 7, 1916

### Mort-Homme Sector
- Germans consolidate gains on northern slope
- French counterattack at dawn (04:30), regain 200 meters
- German counterattack (11:00), retake lost ground
- Pattern established: attack, counterattack, stalemate

### Right Bank
- Relative quiet (German focus shifted to left bank)
- French use lull to strengthen Vaux-Souville defenses

### Casualties (March 7)
- ~3,000 combined (Mort-Homme fighting)

---

## MARCH 8, 1916

### Mort-Homme
- Rain begins (continues intermittently through month)
- Trenches begin to collapse into mud
- German attack renewed (06:00)
- French 67th Division suffers 40% casualties, withdrawn
- Replaced by 11th Division (Noria system)

### Weather Transition
- Rain begins (light)
- Temperature: 4°C to 10°C
- Ground begins to soften

---

## MARCH 9-13, 1916 (Days 18-22)

### Mort-Homme Sector: Continuous Fighting
- Daily German assaults
- Daily French counterattacks
- No significant territorial changes
- Casualties: ~1,500-2,500 per day (both sides)

#### Tactical Pattern
- Germans attack by day (artillery preparation, infantry assault)
- French counterattack by night (recover lost ground)
- Neither side achieves breakthrough
- "Meat grinder" attrition warfare established

### Hill 304 Sector
- German pressure increases on Hill 304 (west of Mort-Homme)
- Preliminary attacks repulsed
- French 42nd Division holds line

### Right Bank
- Local attacks continue (Fort Vaux approaches)
- German 50th Division probes French defenses
- No major assaults

### Weather
- Rain increasing
- Mud deepening
- Temperature: 5°C to 12°C
- Conditions deteriorating rapidly

---

## MARCH 14, 1916 (Day 23)

### **KEY EVENT: MAJOR GERMAN ASSAULT ON HILL 304**

#### 10:00 — Attack Begins
- German VI Reserve Corps attacks Hill 304
- 3-hour artillery preparation (08:00-10:00)
- Infantry assault from north and northwest

#### French Defense
- 42nd Division holds hill
- Intense machine gun fire
- Germans reach lower slopes

#### Result
- Germans gain foothold on northern slope (200-300 meters elevation gain)
- French hold summit (304m)
- Casualties:
  - German: ~1,500
  - French: ~1,200

### Significance
- Two-hill battle now underway (Mort-Homme + Hill 304)
- French artillery observation still intact (both summits held)
- German frustration mounting

---

## MARCH 15-19, 1916 (Days 24-28)

### Both Sectors: Grinding Attrition
- Daily assaults and counterattacks continue
- Mort-Homme and Hill 304 battles simultaneous
- Casualties mounting: ~15,000 French, ~12,000 German (5-day period)

### Conditions
- Continuous rain
- Trenches collapsing
- Wounded drowning in mud-filled shell craters
- Gas attacks (phosgene) by Germans — French masks mostly effective

### Notable Events
- March 16: French 29th Division relieved (50% casualties in 14 days)
- March 17: Guillaume Apollinaire (French poet) wounded by shrapnel near Mort-Homme (survives)
- March 18: German ammunition expenditure: 250,000 shells (single day, left bank only)

### Weather
- Heavy rain (March 17-19)
- Temperature: 6°C to 11°C
- Mud knee-deep in many positions

---

## MARCH 20, 1916 (Day 29)

### **MAJOR GAS ATTACK — MORT-HOMME**

#### 05:00 — Phosgene Gas Release
- Germans release phosgene gas (Green Cross shells)
- Wind favorable (west to east, toward French lines)
- Gas concentrates in ravines and low areas

#### French Response
- Gas alarm sounded
- Men don masks
- Some masks defective or improperly fitted

#### Casualties
- ~400 French gas casualties (50 fatal, 350 hospitalized)
- Germans advance under gas cover
- Gain 300 meters on Mort-Homme eastern slope

#### French Counterattack (Night)
- 22:00: French counterattack to regain lost ground
- Fighting in darkness, with gas masks (visibility near zero)
- Partial success: regain 150 meters
- Exhausted, both sides cease at 02:00 (March 21)

### Casualties (March 20)
- Total: ~2,800 (including gas casualties)

---

## MARCH 21, 1916 (Day 30 / One Month Anniversary)

### Reflection
- One month since Battle of Verdun began
- German objectives unmet:
  - Verdun not taken
  - Fort Douaumont captured but French line holds
  - French artillery still functional on left bank

### Statistics (One Month)
- French casualties: ~89,000 (killed, wounded, missing, captured)
- German casualties: ~81,000
- Total: ~170,000 casualties in 30 days
- Average: ~5,600 casualties per day

### Strategic Assessment (French)
- Pétain's defense holding
- Noria rotation preserving morale
- La Voie Sacrée functioning (7,000 tons supplies/day)
- But: Attrition unsustainable long-term

### Strategic Assessment (German)
- Falkenhayn's "bleeding white" strategy failing (German losses nearly equal French)
- No breakthrough achieved
- French morale not broken
- Crown Prince Wilhelm privately recommends suspension (overruled)

### Weather
- Rain continues
- Temperature: 7°C to 13°C

---

## MARCH 22-26, 1916 (Days 31-35)

### Mort-Homme & Hill 304: Continued Assaults
- German attacks continue daily
- French counterattacks nightly
- Minimal territorial changes
- Casualties: ~10,000 combined (5-day period)

### Tactical Innovations
- Germans experiment with infiltration tactics (small assault groups bypass strongpoints)
- French improve dugout construction (deeper, reinforced)
- Both sides use flares for night signaling

### Notable Events
- March 23: French aviator (unknown) shot down over Mort-Homme
- March 24: German tunneling detected beneath French positions (counter-mine detonated)
- March 25: Noria rotation: 11th Division out, 133rd Division in (Mort-Homme sector)

---

## MARCH 27-31, 1916 (Days 36-40 / End of Month)

### Final Assaults of March
- German offensive intensity decreases slightly (ammunition constraints)
- French hold all key positions: Mort-Homme summit, Hill 304 summit, Vaux-Souville line
- Fighting transitions from massed attacks to localized actions

### Right Bank
- German probes toward Fort Vaux continue
- French strengthen Vaux garrison (anticipating siege)

### Casualties (Final 5 Days of March)
- ~8,000 combined

### Weather (Month End)
- Rain lessens slightly
- Temperature rising: 8°C to 15°C
- Mud remains severe problem

---

## MARCH 1916 — MONTH SUMMARY

### Territorial Changes
- **German Gains**:
  - Northern slopes of Mort-Homme (partial control)
  - Northern slopes of Hill 304 (partial control)
  - Minor advances toward Fort Vaux (right bank)
  - Total: ~2-3 km² additional territory

- **French Retentions**:
  - Summits of Mort-Homme and Hill 304 (critical observation)
  - Fort Vaux intact
  - Verdun city secure
  - Main defensive line intact

### Casualties
- **French**: ~62,000 (March only)
  - Killed: ~18,000
  - Wounded: ~38,000
  - Missing/Captured: ~6,000
- **German**: ~54,000 (March only)
  - Killed: ~16,000
  - Wounded: ~34,000
  - Missing/Captured: ~4,000
- **Total March**: ~116,000 casualties

### Cumulative (Feb 21 - Mar 31)
- **French**: ~151,000
- **German**: ~135,000
- **Total**: ~286,000 casualties in 40 days

## KEY DEVELOPMENTS (MARCH)

### Military
1. **Two-Bank War**: Expansion to left bank doubles battlefield scope
2. **Mort-Homme Focal Point**: Becomes symbol of Verdun's futility
3. **Noria System Functioning**: 40+ French divisions rotated through Verdun
4. **Artillery Dominance**: 70% of casualties from artillery
5. **Gas Warfare**: Phosgene increasingly used; French masks generally effective

### Environmental
1. **Mud**: Becomes dominant factor; inhibits movement, increases suffering
2. **Weather Transition**: Snow → rain → constant mud
3. **Landscape Destruction**: Forests obliterated, villages erased

### Psychological
1. **Attrition Psychology**: Soldiers understand they're in slow-motion massacre
2. **"Mort-Homme" Symbolism**: Name resonates with all combatants
3. **Endurance as Goal**: Victory redefined as "not breaking"

## GAME INTEGRATION NOTES

### Mission Structure (March Campaign)
- **Mission 3**: "The Guns of March" (March 1-6, consolidation after February losses)
- **Mission 4**: "Mort-Homme" (March 6-13, initial assault and defense)
- **Mission 5**: "Hill 304" (March 14-20, two-hill battle)
- **Mission 6**: "The Gas" (March 20-21, gas attack survival and counterattack)
- **Mission 7**: "Endurance" (March 22-31, grinding attrition)

### Gameplay Elements
- **Mud Mechanic**: Movement slowed, stamina drain increased
- **Gas Attacks**: Mask management, limited visibility, panic
- **Artillery Density**: Constant bombardment affects morale, vision, hearing
- **Noria Rotation**: Player character rotated out/back (time jump mechanic)
- **Day/Night Cycle**: German attacks by day, French counterattacks by night

### Environmental Design
- Progressive landscape destruction (compare March 1 vs. March 31)
- Trench degradation (collapse, mud, bodies)
- Weather transitions (snow → rain → mud)
- Mort-Homme topography (hill battles require vertical level design)

### Historical Accuracy
- All dates, casualty figures, unit designations historically accurate
- Weather data from period records
- Commanders' decisions based on documented orders
- Individual soldier accounts (Moreau, Apollinaire) grounded in real testimonies
