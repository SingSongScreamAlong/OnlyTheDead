# VERDUN — APRIL 1916
## Detailed Timeline: The Peak of Attrition

**Phase**: II (cont.) — Left Bank Battles & Continued Right Bank Pressure
**Duration**: April 1-30, 1916 (30 days)
**Key Battles**: Mort-Homme (continued), Hill 304, Haudromont, Douaumont approaches
**Strategic Context**: German offensive slowing; French counterattacks intensifying; both sides exhausted
**Weather**: Spring rains intensify; mud worsens; temperatures rising
**Result**: Stalemate continues; highest casualty rates of entire battle

---

## APRIL 1, 1916 (Day 41 of Battle)

### Left Bank - Mort-Homme
- Germans control northern slopes, French hold summit and southern approaches
- Daily artillery exchanges continue (~15,000 shells/day, both sides)
- No major infantry assaults (both sides regrouping after March exhaustion)

### Right Bank
- German probes toward Haudromont Quarry
- French strengthen Douaumont approaches (prevent further German advance)

### Weather
- Spring rain begins
- Temperature: 8°C to 16°C
- Mud deepening

### Noria Rotation
- 133rd Division (Mort-Homme sector) relieved after 15 days
- Replaced by 34th Division

---

## APRIL 2-8, 1916 (Days 42-48)

### Tactical Pause
- Both armies exhausted after 6 weeks continuous fighting
- German High Command debates: continue Verdun offensive vs. suspend
- Crown Prince Wilhelm advocates suspension (rejected by Falkenhayn)
- French: Pétain reinforces defensive positions, prepares limited counterattacks

### Artillery Duels
- Continuous counter-battery fire
- German ammunition consumption constrained (diverting resources to other fronts)
- French artillery increasingly effective (Nivelle's centralized command working)

### Casualties (April 1-8)
- Relatively "quiet" period: ~12,000 total (both sides)
- Daily average: ~1,500 (vs. March average of ~3,800/day)

### Weather
- Intermittent rain
- Temperature: 10°C to 18°C
- Mud remains severe problem

---

## APRIL 9, 1916 (Day 49)

### **KEY EVENT: FRENCH COUNTERATTACK ON MORT-HOMME**

#### 04:30 — French Offensive Begins
- **Commander**: Gen. Robert Nivelle (artillery), Gen. Guyot de Salins (34th Division, infantry)
- **Objective**: Recapture northern slopes of Mort-Homme
- **Method**: Rolling barrage (Nivelle's innovation)
  - Artillery advances 100m every 5 minutes
  - Infantry follows 50m behind barrage
- **Forces**: ~15,000 French infantry, 200 guns

#### Assault
- French advance under cover of artillery curtain
- Initial success: Gain 400m, reach German trenches
- Hand-to-hand combat in captured positions
- German counterattack (11:00): Reinforcements arrive
- French hold 200m of gains

#### Result
- French tactical success (territory gained)
- Demonstrates Nivelle's rolling barrage effectiveness
- German losses: ~1,800
- French losses: ~1,500

#### Significance
- First significant French territorial gain since February
- Boosts French morale
- Pétain issues Order of the Day (April 10): "On les aura!"

---

## APRIL 10, 1916

### Aftermath of April 9 Counterattack
- Germans attempt to retake lost ground (unsuccessful)
- French consolidate positions
- Both sides exhausted

### Pétain's Order of the Day
- Famous order issued: Praise for troops, "On les aura!" phrase
- Widely distributed, becomes propaganda slogan
- Genuine boost to morale

---

## APRIL 11-14, 1916 (Days 51-54)

### Right Bank Activity Increases
- German focus shifts back to right bank (left bank stalemate)
- Probes toward Haudromont Quarry intensify
- Fort Douaumont vicinity: German consolidation

### Left Bank
- Local attacks and counterattacks continue
- No major operations
- Casualties: ~8,000 (4-day period)

### Weather
- Heavy rain (April 12-13)
- Trenches flooding
- Temperature: 9°C to 15°C

---

## APRIL 15, 1916 (Day 55)

### German Assault on Haudromont Quarry (Right Bank)

#### 10:00 — Attack Begins
- **German Forces**: Elements of 50th Division
- **Objective**: Capture Haudromont Quarry (approaches to Douaumont sector)
- **Method**: Artillery preparation (4 hours), infantry assault

#### Battle
- French defenders (XX Corps, 37th Division): ~3,000 men
- German assault: ~5,000 men
- Fighting in quarry: Vertical terrain, brutal close combat
- Germans capture northern section of quarry
- French hold southern section

#### Result
- Partial German success
- Casualties:
  - German: ~900
  - French: ~1,200

---

## APRIL 16-20, 1916 (Days 56-60)

### Haudromont Quarry: Continued Fighting
- Germans attempt to complete capture (April 16-17)
- French counterattacks (April 18-19)
- Quarry divided: German north, French south
- Becomes no-man's-land (neither side fully controls)

### Left Bank
- Artillery duels continue
- Small-scale raids (both sides)
- Casualties: ~10,000 (5-day period)

### Weather
- Rain lessens
- Temperature rising: 11°C to 19°C
- Ground begins to dry slightly (but still muddy)

---

## APRIL 21, 1916 (Day 61 / Two Months)

### Two-Month Anniversary
- February 21 - April 21: 61 days continuous battle
- **Cumulative Casualties**:
  - French: ~179,000 (89k Feb-Mar, 90k April 1-21)
  - German: ~159,000 (81k Feb-Mar, 78k April 1-21)
  - Total: ~338,000 casualties in 61 days
  - Average: ~5,540 casualties per day

### Strategic Assessment (French)
- Verdun line holding
- German offensive losing momentum
- But: Attrition rate unsustainable
- Pétain requests more divisions

### Strategic Assessment (German)
- Objectives not achieved:
  - Verdun not taken
  - French Army not bled to breaking point (German casualties nearly equal)
- Falkenhayn under pressure to show results
- Decision: Intensify offensive one more time

---

## APRIL 22-25, 1916 (Days 62-65)

### German Preparation for Renewed Offensive
- Artillery reinforcements moved to Verdun sector
- Fresh divisions: 11th Bavarian Division, 5th Division
- Target: Final push toward Fort Vaux

### French Preparation
- Vaux garrison reinforced (anticipating siege)
- Artillery positioned to cover Vaux approaches
- Noria rotation: Multiple divisions cycled

### Notable Event (April 22)
- Sous-Lt. Henri Moreau (FR002) observes lark singing during bombardment
  - Records in diary: "The birds don't care about the war"
  - Small moment of beauty amid horror

---

## APRIL 26, 1916 (Day 66)

### Left Bank: Hill 304 Assault Intensifies

#### 14:00 — Major German Attack on Hill 304
- **German Forces**: VI Reserve Corps (~20,000 men)
- **Objective**: Capture Hill 304 summit (eliminate French artillery observation)
- **Method**: Massive artillery preparation (8 hours, 08:00-14:00)

#### Battle
- French defenders (42nd Division): ~8,000 men
- Germans advance from northwest
- Reach midpoint of hill (250m elevation)
- French hold summit (304m)
- Fighting continues into night

#### Result
- German gains: Lower and middle slopes
- French retains summit (barely)
- Casualties:
  - German: ~2,500
  - French: ~3,200

---

## APRIL 27-30, 1916 (Days 67-70 / End of Month)

### Hill 304: Culmination
- Germans push for summit (April 27-29)
- French counterattacks (nightly)
- Hill 304 becomes mirror of Mort-Homme: Germans hold slopes, French hold summit (barely)
- Casualties mounting catastrophically: ~15,000 (4-day period)

### Right Bank
- German pressure continues toward Vaux
- Local gains: Caillette Wood, approaches to Vaux
- Fort Vaux now within German artillery range

### Weather (Month End)
- Spring weather stabilizing
- Temperature: 12°C to 21°C
- Mud beginning to dry (but still significant)

### Cumulative (Feb 21 - Apr 30)
- **French**: ~248,000
- **German**: ~221,000
- **Total**: ~469,000 casualties in 70 days

## TACTICAL DEVELOPMENTS (APRIL)

### French Innovations
1. **Rolling Barrage** (April 9 counterattack):
   - Artillery walks forward in timed increments
   - Infantry follows close behind
   - Highly effective (German defenses overwhelmed)
   - Becomes standard French tactic (Nivelle's legacy)

2. **Improved Counter-Battery**:
   - French artillery increasingly accurate
   - Aerial observation improving
   - German guns suppressed more effectively

3. **Noria System Refined**:
   - 15-day rotation strictly enforced
   - Prevents total psychological collapse
   - 70+ divisions now rotated through Verdun

### German Tactics
1. **Attrition Focus**:
   - Less emphasis on breakthrough
   - More on inflicting maximum French casualties
   - "Bleeding white" strategy continues (but backfiring—German casualties nearly equal)

2. **Artillery Dependency**:
   - Infantry assaults only after extended bombardment
   - Effectiveness declining (French dugouts deeper, better constructed)

3. **Flamethrowers**:
   - Increased use in trench fighting
   - Psychologically terrifying but tactically limited (short range, vulnerable)

## ENVIRONMENTAL CONDITIONS (APRIL)

### Weather Transition
- Winter → Spring
- Snow/ice → Rain/mud
- Temperatures rising (8°C to 21°C range)
- Rain frequent but less intense than March

### Battlefield Conditions
- **Mud**: Still severe but beginning to dry
- **Corpses**: Thousands unburied, decomposing in spring warmth
- **Disease**: Dysentery, trench fever, respiratory infections epidemic
- **Water**: Contaminated (corpses, chemicals, sewage)
- **Air Quality**: Smoke, gas residue, decay stench constant

### Psychological Impact
- **Shell Shock Cases**: Increasing exponentially
  - April: ~1,200 French hospitalized for shell shock (vs. ~800 March)
  - Symptoms: Mutism, paralysis, catatonia, violent outbursts
  - Treatment: Primitive (rest, sedation, electrical "therapy")

- **Demoralization**:
  - Both armies exhausted
  - Soldiers' letters increasingly fatalistic
  - "We cannot win, but we must endure"

## CASUALTY ANALYSIS (APRIL)

### Why April Had Highest Casualty Rates

1. **Peak Intensity**: Both sides committed maximum forces
2. **Improved Weather**: Spring conditions enabled more operations (vs. winter slowdown)
3. **Two-Front War**: Fighting on both banks simultaneously
4. **Desperation**: Germans pushing for breakthrough before Somme offensive
5. **French Counterattacks**: Nivelle's aggressive doctrine (counterattack always)
6. **Artillery Density**: More guns per kilometer than any previous battle in history

### April as Turning Point
- German offensive reaches culmination
- After April, German attacks decrease in frequency and intensity
- French increasingly take initiative (counterattacks)
- May-June: German focus shifts to right bank only (Fort Vaux siege)
- Verdun transitions from German offensive to mutual attrition stalemate

---

## THEMATIC SIGNIFICANCE (APRIL)

April 1916 represents Verdun's **apex of futility**:
- Highest casualties for minimal territorial change
- Both sides exhausted but neither can stop
- Rolling barrage offers hope (French can attack successfully) but changes nothing strategically
- Two-month mark reveals grim truth: This will not end soon
- Soldiers' writings (Moreau, Steinbrecher) increasingly nihilistic
- Medical system strained to breaking (Nurse Dubois letter)
- Even chaplains losing faith (Father Bernard)

**April is the month Verdun becomes not a battle but a condition**—a permanent state of violence that simply *is*, with no end imaginable.
